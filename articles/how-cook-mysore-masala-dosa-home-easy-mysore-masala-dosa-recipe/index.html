How to Cook Mysore Masala Dosa at Home – Easy Mysore Masala Dosa Recipe

By Reddamma D | Published: Sep 4, 2026 8:12 AM IST
By Reddamma D , 4 September 2026
How to Cook Mysore Masala Dosa at Home – Easy Mysore Masala Dosa Recipe

Have you ever had a crispy Mysore Masala Dosa at a South Indian restaurant and wondered if you could make the same thing at home? You absolutely can!

Mysore Masala Dosa is a tasty South Indian dish made with a thin, crispy dosa, spicy red chutney, and soft potato masala. It is a little different from a regular masala dosa because of the special red chutney spread inside the dosa. This chutney gives the dosa its lovely red color and spicy taste.

Don't worry if you have never made dosa before. With a little practice, you can make a delicious Mysore Masala Dosa at home. The batter needs some time to soak and ferment, but the actual cooking is quite simple.

Let's see how to make it step by step.

Ingredients You Need

1. For the Dosa Batter

  • 2 cups rice
  • ½ cup urad dal
  • 1 tablespoon poha
  • ½ teaspoon fenugreek seeds
  • Salt to taste
  • Water as needed
  • Oil or ghee for cooking

2. For the Potato Masala

  • 4 medium potatoes
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 1–2 green chilies, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 tablespoon chana dal
  • 8–10 curry leaves
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • Salt to taste
  • A little chopped coriander

3. For the Red Chutney

  • 5–6 dried red chilies
  • 2 tablespoons roasted gram
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 tablespoon grated coconut
  • A small piece of tamarind
  • ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
  • Salt to taste
  • A little water

Step 1: Make the Dosa Batter

The first thing you need is dosa batter. Good batter is the secret to making a crispy and tasty dosa.

  • Wash the rice and urad dal well. Soak the rice with fenugreek seeds in one bowl and the urad dal in another bowl. Let them soak for about 4–6 hours.
  • After soaking, drain the water. Grind the urad dal until it becomes soft and fluffy. Then grind the rice with the poha until you get a smooth batter.
  • Mix both batters in a large bowl. Add a little water if needed. The batter should be thick but easy to pour.
  • Cover the bowl and keep it in a warm place for about 8–12 hours. This allows the batter to ferment.
  • When the batter is ready, you will notice that it has become slightly bigger and has tiny bubbles. It may also have a mild sour smell. This is a good sign!
  • Add salt and mix the batter gently. If it is too thick, add a little water.

Step 2: Make the Potato Masala

Now let's make the filling.

  • First, boil the potatoes until they are soft. Peel them and break them into small pieces. You don't have to mash them completely.
  • Heat oil in a pan. Add mustard seeds and let them crackle. Then add cumin seeds, chana dal, curry leaves, and chopped green chilies.
  • Add the sliced onion and cook until it becomes soft.
  • Next, add turmeric powder and salt. Put in the boiled potatoes and mix everything together.
  • If the potato mixture looks dry, add a few tablespoons of water. Cook it for another 2–3 minutes.
  • Finally, add some fresh coriander.

Your potato masala is ready!

Step 3: Make the Spicy Red Chutney

This is the part that makes Mysore Masala Dosa different from a regular masala dosa.

  • Soak the dried red chilies in warm water for about 15 minutes. This makes them soft and easier to blend.
  • Drain the water and put the chilies into a blender. Add roasted gram, garlic, onion, coconut, tamarind, cumin seeds, and salt.
  • Add a little water and blend everything into a smooth paste.
  • Taste the chutney. If it is too spicy for you, simply add a little more coconut or roasted gram.

The chutney should be thick enough to spread easily on the dosa.

Step 4: Cook the Mysore Masala Dosa

Now comes the fun part — making the dosa!

  • Place a dosa tawa or flat pan on the stove and heat it on medium heat.
  • Take a ladleful of dosa batter and pour it in the middle of the hot tawa.
  • Using the back of the ladle, spread the batter in circles. Try to make the dosa thin. Don't worry if your first dosa doesn't look perfect. It takes a little practice!
  • Drizzle some oil or ghee around the edges. Let the dosa cook until the bottom turns golden and crispy.
  • Now spread a thin layer of the red chutney all over the dosa.
  • Place some potato masala in the middle. You can spread it a little or leave it in the center.
  • Let it cook for another minute. When the dosa looks crispy and golden, fold it in half or roll it up.

Your homemade Mysore Masala Dosa is ready!

Tips for a Crispy Mysore Masala Dosa

Making crispy dosa becomes easier once you learn a few simple tricks.

  • Make sure the batter is fermented well. Fermentation helps give the dosa a light texture and good taste.
  • Don't make the batter too thick. If the batter doesn't spread easily, add a little water.
  • Keep the tawa at the right temperature. If it is too hot, the batter will be difficult to spread. If it is too cold, the dosa may not become crispy.
  • Don't use too much red chutney. A thin layer is enough to give the dosa plenty of flavor.
  • Be patient with your first dosa. Your first one may not be perfect, and that's completely normal. After making a few dosas, you'll get the hang of it.
  • Try ghee instead of oil. A little ghee can make the dosa smell and taste even better.

What to Serve With Mysore Masala Dosa

Mysore Masala Dosa tastes best when you eat it hot and crispy.

You can serve it with coconut chutney and hot sambar. You can also serve it with tomato chutney or peanut chutney if you like.

If you're making a South Indian breakfast at home, a cup of hot filter coffee goes really well with dosa too.

Can You Make Mysore Masala Dosa Without a Cast-Iron Tawa?

Yes! You can use a non-stick pan if that's what you have at home.

A cast-iron tawa can give you a very crispy dosa, but it may take some practice to use it properly. If you're a beginner, a good non-stick pan can make things easier.

Can You Make the Batter Ahead of Time?

Yes. In fact, making the batter ahead of time makes breakfast much easier.

Once the batter has fermented, you can keep it in the refrigerator. When you want to make dosa, take out the amount you need and let it come closer to room temperature. Add a little water if the batter has become too thick.

Final Thoughts

Making Mysore Masala Dosa at home may look difficult at first, but it is actually quite simple once you understand the steps.

The most important things are a well-fermented dosa batter, a tasty potato filling, and the spicy red chutney. Once these are ready, making the dosa is easy.

Don't worry if your first dosa is not perfectly round or crispy. Keep trying, and you'll get better each time.

So the next time you are craving restaurant-style Mysore Masala Dosa, don't order it. Make it at home instead! Heat your tawa, spread the batter, add the spicy chutney and potato masala, and enjoy a hot, crispy dosa with your family.
