Karnataka-Style Vegetable Sagu Recipe: A Delicious South Indian Curry for Poori, Dosa and Idli

Karnataka-Style Vegetable Sagu Recipe: A Delicious South Indian Curry for Poori, Dosa and Idli

Karnataka-style vegetable sagu is a popular South Indian curry known for its creamy coconut-based gravy, aromatic spices and colourful combination of vegetables. This comforting dish is commonly served in Karnataka homes and restaurants alongside crispy poori, soft set dosa, rava idli and chapati. Its distinctive flavour comes from freshly ground masala, which gives the gravy a rich texture and a wonderful balance of spices.

If you have ever enjoyed vegetable sagu at a Bengaluru breakfast restaurant, you may have wondered how to recreate that familiar taste at home. The good news is that you do not need complicated ingredients or professional cooking skills. With a handful of fresh vegetables, coconut, green chillies and everyday spices, you can prepare delicious homemade vegetable sagu for your family.

This easy Karnataka-style vegetable sagu recipe is suitable for breakfast, lunch or dinner. You can also adjust the vegetables and spice levels according to your family's preferences.

What Makes Karnataka-Style Vegetable Sagu Special?

Vegetable sagu, also known as vegetable saagu, is a traditional Karnataka-style curry prepared using mixed vegetables and a freshly ground spice paste. Although it resembles vegetable kurma, its flavour depends on the particular combination of coconut, coriander, cumin, ginger, green chillies and whole spices used in the masala.

The vegetables provide different textures, while the ground coconut mixture gives the gravy its characteristic thickness. Some versions include mint leaves, roasted gram, poppy seeds or cashews to create a richer taste.

Every household may have its own version of sagu. Some cooks prefer a green-coloured gravy made with coriander leaves, while others use additional red chillies or tomatoes for a different appearance and flavour.

One of the best things about this Karnataka breakfast recipe is its versatility. You can prepare it with commonly available vegetables and serve it with several Indian dishes.

Ingredients for Karnataka-Style Vegetable Sagu

Preparation time: 15 minutes
Cooking time: 25 minutes
Total time: Approximately 40 minutes
Servings: 4 people
Cuisine: Karnataka, South Indian
Difficulty: Easy

For the Vegetable Sagu

  • 1 medium potato, peeled and diced
  • 1 medium carrot, chopped
  • ½ cup green beans, chopped
  • ½ cup green peas
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 1 small tomato, chopped (optional)
  • ¼ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • ½ teaspoon mustard seeds
  • ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 teaspoon urad dal
  • 8–10 curry leaves
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • Salt to taste
  • Water as required
  • Fresh coriander leaves for garnishing

For the Ground Sagu Masala

  • ½ cup freshly grated coconut
  • 2 tablespoons roasted gram (putani)
  • 1 teaspoon poppy seeds
  • 1 tablespoon coriander seeds
  • ½ teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 2–3 green chillies
  • 1-inch piece of ginger
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 small cinnamon stick
  • 2 cloves
  • 1 cardamom pod
  • A small handful of fresh coriander leaves
  • 4–5 mint leaves (optional)
  • Water as required for grinding

How to Make Karnataka-Style Vegetable Sagu at Home

Step 1: Prepare the Vegetables

Wash all the vegetables thoroughly under running water. Peel the potato and carrot, then chop them into small, evenly sized pieces. Cut the beans into short sections and keep the green peas ready.

Try to cut the vegetables into similar sizes so they cook evenly. You can also add cauliflower or knol khol if you want to include more variety.

Steam the vegetables or pressure-cook them with a little water until they are tender but still hold their shape. Avoid overcooking them, as the vegetables should retain some texture in the finished curry.

Step 2: Prepare the Fresh Sagu Masala

Add grated coconut, roasted gram, poppy seeds, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, green chillies, ginger, garlic, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom and fresh coriander leaves to a mixer grinder.

Add mint leaves if you prefer a more herbaceous flavour. Pour in a little water and grind everything into a smooth, thick paste.

The ground masala is the heart of this vegetable sagu recipe. Grinding the ingredients finely helps create a creamy gravy that coats the vegetables evenly.

If the mixture becomes too thick to grind, add a little more water. Avoid adding too much water at once, as the masala should remain concentrated.

Step 3: Prepare the Tempering

Heat oil in a heavy-bottomed kadai or deep pan over medium heat. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter. Next, add cumin seeds, urad dal and curry leaves.

Let the urad dal turn lightly golden, taking care not to burn it. Add the chopped onion and sauté until it becomes soft and translucent.

If you are using tomato, add it at this stage and cook until it softens. The onion and tomato create a flavourful base for the coconut masala.

Step 4: Cook the Ground Masala

Add the freshly prepared sagu masala to the pan. Stir continuously and cook over medium-low heat for approximately 3–4 minutes.

You should notice the aroma of the coconut, coriander, ginger and whole spices becoming more pronounced as the masala cooks.

Do not rush this step. Allowing the raw smell of the ground ingredients to disappear helps develop a more balanced flavour.

If the masala begins sticking to the bottom of the pan, add a small splash of water and continue stirring.

Step 5: Add the Cooked Vegetables

Add the cooked potato, carrot, beans and green peas to the pan. Sprinkle in turmeric powder and salt according to your taste.

Mix gently so the vegetables are coated with the masala. Pour in enough water to achieve your preferred consistency.

For a thicker gravy to serve with poori, use less water. If you plan to serve the sagu with set dosa or rava idli, a slightly looser consistency may be more convenient.

Step 6: Simmer Until the Flavours Combine

Cover the pan partially and simmer the curry over low heat for about 5–8 minutes. Stir occasionally to prevent the masala from sticking.

As the curry simmers, the flavours of the spices, coconut and vegetables come together. The gravy should become smooth and aromatic, with the vegetables distributed evenly throughout.

Taste the sagu and adjust the salt or spice level if necessary. Turn off the heat and garnish with freshly chopped coriander leaves.

Your homemade Karnataka-style vegetable sagu is now ready to serve.

Tips for Making Restaurant-Style Vegetable Sagu

Use fresh coconut: Freshly grated coconut provides a pleasant flavour and creamy consistency. Frozen grated coconut can also be used after thawing.

Grind the masala smoothly: A finely ground paste gives the curry a more uniform texture. Add water gradually while grinding.

Avoid overcooking the vegetables: Soft vegetables that retain their shape make the finished dish more appealing and enjoyable to eat.

Adjust the green chillies: Use two green chillies for a milder curry or increase the quantity if your family enjoys spicier food.

Balance the gravy consistency: The curry thickens as it cools. Add a little hot water and simmer briefly if it becomes too thick.

Experiment with seasonal vegetables: Cauliflower, knol khol and other suitable vegetables can be added alongside the usual carrot, beans, potato and peas.

Prepare the masala ahead of time: You can grind the masala shortly before cooking and refrigerate it in a covered container for convenient meal preparation.

What to Serve with Karnataka Vegetable Sagu

Vegetable sagu pairs particularly well with traditional Karnataka breakfast dishes. The creamy gravy complements both crisp and soft textures.

  • Poori: Hot, puffed pooris are a classic accompaniment. Tear a piece of poori and scoop up the thick vegetable gravy for a satisfying combination.
  • Set dosa: Soft, spongy set dosa absorbs the flavourful masala beautifully and makes an excellent breakfast.
  • Rava idli: The slightly grainy texture of rava idli works well with the smooth coconut-based curry.
  • Plain dosa: Serve the sagu alongside dosa for a filling South Indian meal.
  • Chapati or paratha: Vegetable sagu can also be served as a curry for everyday lunch or dinner.
  • Steamed rice: A slightly thinner version can be enjoyed with rice when you want a simple, comforting meal.

For a traditional breakfast spread, serve vegetable sagu with set dosa or poori, coconut chutney and a cup of South Indian filter coffee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make vegetable sagu without poppy seeds?

Yes. You can omit poppy seeds if they are unavailable. Roasted gram and coconut will still help provide body and creaminess to the gravy, although the flavour and texture may vary slightly.

Can I make Karnataka-style vegetable sagu without onion and garlic?

Yes. Leave out the onion and garlic if you prefer. You can prepare the tempering with mustard seeds, cumin seeds, curry leaves and oil, then continue with the ground masala and vegetables.

How can I make vegetable sagu thicker?

Grind the coconut masala finely and use less water while cooking. You can also mash a few cooked potato pieces into the gravy to help thicken it naturally.

How should leftover vegetable sagu be stored?

Allow the curry to cool, transfer it to an airtight container and refrigerate promptly. For best quality, consume it within 2–3 days. Reheat thoroughly before serving, adding a little water if needed. Avoid repeatedly reheating the entire batch.

Is vegetable sagu suitable for breakfast?

Absolutely. Vegetable sagu is commonly served for breakfast in Karnataka, particularly with poori, set dosa and rava idli. It can also be enjoyed for lunch or dinner.

Final Thoughts

Karnataka-style vegetable sagu is a wonderful example of how fresh vegetables and a carefully prepared spice paste can create a flavourful homemade curry. Its creamy coconut base, fragrant spices and colourful vegetables make it a versatile accompaniment for many South Indian favourites.

Whether you are recreating a Bengaluru-style breakfast at home or looking for a new curry to serve with chapati, this easy vegetable sagu recipe is worth trying. Adjust the vegetables, chillies and gravy consistency to suit your taste, and enjoy it fresh with your favourite accompaniment.

Prepare a batch for your next family breakfast and discover why vegetable sagu continues to be a much-loved part of Karnataka's food culture.
