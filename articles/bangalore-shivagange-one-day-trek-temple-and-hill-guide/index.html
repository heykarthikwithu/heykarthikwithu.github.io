Bangalore to Shivagange: One-Day Trek, Temple and Hill Guide

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Oct 12, 2026 8:55 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 12 October 2026
Bangalore to Shivagange: One-Day Trek, Temple and Hill Guide

Just a few hours away from the city’s busy roads and crowded streets, Shivagange offers a refreshing combination of trekking, ancient temples and beautiful hilltop views. Located near Dabaspet in Karnataka’s Bengaluru Rural district, this popular destination is ideal for travellers looking for a one-day trip from Bangalore without travelling too far.

The Shivagange trek is not just about reaching the summit. The route takes you past religious shrines, rocky steps, traditional temple structures and natural formations that make the climb interesting from beginning to end. Whether you are planning a weekend adventure with friends, a family outing or a pilgrimage, Shivagange offers several reasons to make an early start from Bangalore.

Here is a practical guide to planning your Bangalore to Shivagange trip, including how to reach the hill, what to see, trekking tips and an itinerary for the day.

Where Is Shivagange?

Shivagange, also known as Shivaganga, is a hill and pilgrimage destination in Nelamangala taluk of Bengaluru Rural district, Karnataka. Rising approximately 1,368 metres above sea level, the hill is known for its distinctive rocky landscape and religious significance.

The hill is associated with Lord Shiva and is popularly known as Dakshina Kashi, or the Kashi of the South. Its shape resembles a Shivalinga, while a sacred spring associated with the name Ganga adds to its spiritual importance.

Shivagange is also a popular trekking destination because of its stone steps, steep sections and panoramic views from higher elevations. This combination of adventure and spirituality makes it one of the interesting places to visit near Bangalore for a day trip. 

Bangalore to Shivagange Distance and How to Reach

Shivagange is approximately 50–60 km from Bangalore, depending on your starting point and the route you take. The journey generally takes around 1.5–2 hours by road, although weekend traffic and road conditions can affect travel time.

By car or bike: Driving is one of the most convenient ways to reach Shivagange. From Bangalore, head towards Nelamangala and continue towards Dabaspet using the Tumakuru highway corridor. Follow the local road signs towards Shivagange village and the hill entrance.

Starting early helps you avoid much of the city traffic and gives you more time to complete the trek before the afternoon heat.

By bus: Karnataka State Road Transport Corporation and other bus services operate along routes connecting Bangalore with nearby towns. You can travel towards Dabaspet and check local transport availability for the final stretch to Shivagange. Depending on the service, an additional auto or taxi ride may be necessary.

By train: Rail services to nearby stations may be useful, but the last-mile journey still needs planning. Check current train schedules and local transport before choosing this option.

For a first visit, a car or bike is generally the simpler choice because it allows greater flexibility with the return journey.

Best Time to Visit Shivagange

The cooler months from October to February are generally comfortable for trekking at Shivagange. Early mornings are preferable throughout the year, particularly during warmer months when climbing the exposed rocky sections can become tiring.

The post-monsoon landscape can be attractive, with greener surroundings and pleasant weather. However, rain and moisture can make stone steps and smooth rock surfaces slippery. Exercise additional caution during and immediately after rainfall.

Weekdays may offer a quieter experience than weekends and public holidays. If you plan to visit during a festival or a major religious occasion, expect larger crowds around the temples.

An early start is especially important if you want to explore the temples and complete the climb without rushing.

Shivagange Trek: What to Expect

The Shivagange trek combines constructed steps, rocky terrain and steep uphill sections. Although the route is marked and includes railings in some exposed areas, it should not be mistaken for an effortless walk.

The ascent can be tiring for people who are not accustomed to physical activity. Take regular breaks, drink water and move at a comfortable pace rather than trying to keep up with faster trekkers.

As you climb, you will pass temple structures and viewpoints that provide opportunities to rest and appreciate the surroundings. The higher sections become steeper and narrower, requiring extra care with every step.

Allow several hours for the overall trekking experience, including the ascent, time spent exploring the summit and the descent. The exact duration depends on your fitness, the number of breaks you take and crowd conditions.

Good-grip footwear is essential. Avoid slippers, smooth-soled sandals and footwear that makes you feel unstable on stone steps.

Temples and Places to See at Shivagange

One of the main attractions of Shivagange is the opportunity to combine a hill trek with visits to several religious sites.

Gangadhareshwara Temple: This temple is closely associated with Lord Shiva and is among the important pilgrimage sites at Shivagange. Its religious significance makes it a natural stop for visitors interested in the area's spiritual heritage.

Honnammadevi Temple: Another important shrine in the area, this temple is associated with local religious traditions and adds to Shivagange's reputation as a pilgrimage destination.

Olakal Teertha: This sacred water site is one of the notable attractions associated with the hill. Visitors interested in the religious traditions of Shivagange often include it in their exploration.

Patala Gange: Known for its association with sacred water and the hill's rocky landscape, Patala Gange is another recognised attraction. Access and conditions may vary, so follow local instructions rather than attempting to enter restricted or unsafe areas.

Nandi statue and summit: The upper part of the hill features a prominent Nandi figure and striking rocky surroundings. Reaching this area is a highlight for many trekkers, although the final stretch requires particular care.

These attractions give Shivagange a character different from that of a conventional hill viewpoint. The journey is as much about the temples and cultural heritage as it is about the climb.

Things to Carry for the Shivagange Trek

A little preparation can make the trek considerably more comfortable. Carry the following essentials:

  • Comfortable trekking shoes with good grip.
  • At least 1–2 litres of drinking water, adjusted for weather and personal needs.
  • A small backpack with light snacks.
  • A cap, sunglasses and sunscreen.
  • A light rain jacket during the monsoon season.
  • Personal medication and a basic first-aid kit.
  • A fully charged mobile phone and power bank.
  • Some cash for local purchases and transport.

Monkeys are commonly seen around Shivagange. Keep food inside your bag, avoid feeding them and do not try to retrieve items by approaching them. Carry back your plastic waste and respect the temple surroundings.

Important Tips Before Visiting Shivagange

Although Shivagange is a popular destination for day trips, the steep sections require reasonable mobility and physical stamina. Travellers with knee problems, balance difficulties or other mobility limitations should assess whether the climb is suitable for them.

Keep children close on narrow sections and avoid risky shortcuts or climbing away from the designated route. Do not continue towards exposed areas during rain, strong winds or other unsafe conditions.

Temple access, facilities and local rules can change. Check current conditions before departure, and follow instructions from temple staff and local authorities. Do not assume that every attraction will be accessible throughout the day.

If you are travelling with older family members, you can still enjoy the accessible temple areas without necessarily attempting the entire ascent.

Is Shivagange Worth a One-Day Trip from Bangalore?

Shivagange is a worthwhile choice for travellers who want more than a conventional weekend outing. It combines a rewarding physical challenge with religious heritage, distinctive rock formations and views of the surrounding countryside.

The destination is particularly appealing to people who enjoy short road trips, outdoor activities and exploring historic temples. However, the steep climb means that planning your timing, carrying suitable footwear and knowing your physical limits are important.

For a memorable Bangalore to Shivagange one-day trip, start early, explore the temples at a relaxed pace and leave enough time for a careful descent. The best experience comes from enjoying the journey rather than treating the summit as a race to the top.
