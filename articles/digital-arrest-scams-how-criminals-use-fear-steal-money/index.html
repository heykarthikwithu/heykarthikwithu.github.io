Digital Arrest Scams: How Criminals Use Fear to Steal Money

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Sep 16, 2026 8:32 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 16 September 2026
Digital Arrest Scams: How Criminals Use Fear to Steal Money

Imagine receiving a phone call from someone claiming to be a police officer. They tell you that your Aadhaar card has been linked to a serious crime, your bank account is under investigation, or a parcel in your name contains illegal items. Before you have time to understand what is happening, they threaten to arrest you.

Then comes the shocking instruction: stay on a video call, follow their orders, and transfer money to prove your innocence.

This is how a digital arrest scam works.

In recent years, cybercriminals in India have increasingly used fake police calls, video conferences, and threats of legal action to frighten people into handing over their money. These scams are designed to look convincing, but understanding how they work can help you avoid becoming a victim.

What Is a Digital Arrest Scam?

A digital arrest scam is a type of online fraud in which criminals pretend to be police officers, government officials, or representatives of investigating agencies. They contact people through phone calls, WhatsApp, or video calls and falsely accuse them of being involved in criminal activities.

The scammer may claim to represent the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), Mumbai Police, the Narcotics Control Bureau, the Reserve Bank of India, or another government department.

They might tell you that:

  • Your Aadhaar card has been used in a criminal case.
  • A parcel carrying illegal goods has been sent in your name.
  • Your bank account is connected to money laundering.
  • Your mobile number has been involved in suspicious activities.
  • A family member has been arrested or is facing criminal charges.

After making these accusations, the criminals threaten the victim with arrest or legal action unless they follow instructions or pay money.

However, digital arrest is not a legal form of arrest in India. Genuine law enforcement agencies do not arrest people through video calls or demand money to make a criminal case disappear.

How Do Digital Arrest Scams Work?

Digital arrest scams usually follow a pattern. Criminals carefully create fear and confusion before asking their victims for money.

1. The Scam Begins With an Unexpected Call

The first call may come from someone claiming to work for a courier company, telecom provider, bank, or government agency.

For example, the caller might say that a parcel connected to your name has been seized at an airport. They may claim that the parcel contains illegal substances or valuable items.

The caller then says that your phone number or identity documents have been linked to the shipment.

Sometimes, the scam begins with a claim that your mobile connection will be disconnected because of suspicious activity.

Although the initial story may sound unrelated to the police, the caller eventually transfers you to another person who claims to be an investigating officer.

2. Criminals Use Fear to Control Their Victims

Once the victim is connected to the supposed officer, the conversation becomes more threatening.

The scammer may accuse the victim of money laundering, identity theft, or involvement in a criminal network. They may mention arrest warrants, court cases, or imprisonment.

They often speak confidently and use complicated legal terms to make the situation appear genuine.

The victim may be told not to disconnect the call, contact family members, or discuss the matter with anyone.

The purpose is to isolate the victim and prevent them from checking whether the allegations are real.

3. Fake Police Officers Appear on Video Calls

In many digital arrest scams, fraudsters use video calls to make their deception more believable.

They may appear in police uniforms, sit in front of a background resembling a police station, or show fake identity cards and official-looking documents.

Some criminals use multiple people during the same call. One person may act as a police officer, another as a senior official, and another as a legal representative.

This creates the impression that a serious investigation is taking place.

In reality, these people are working together to manipulate the victim.

A video call does not prove that someone is a genuine police officer. Criminals can use edited videos, fake backgrounds, stolen images, and other technology to create a convincing appearance.

4. The Victim Is Asked to Transfer Money

After creating enough fear, the scammer makes a financial demand.

The victim may be told to transfer money for:

  • Police verification.
  • A security deposit.
  • Bank account clearance.
  • Anti-money-laundering checks.
  • Bail or settlement.
  • Cancellation of an arrest warrant.

The scammer may promise that the money will be returned after the investigation is completed.

This is a lie designed to encourage the victim to make the payment.

Once the first payment is made, the criminals may demand more money by claiming that another problem has been discovered.

Some victims lose their savings because they continue paying in the hope of avoiding arrest.

Why Are Digital Arrest Scams So Effective?

The success of these scams depends largely on psychological pressure.

Many people become nervous when they hear the words “police,” “court,” or “criminal case.” Scammers take advantage of this fear and make the situation appear urgent.

They may know basic information about the victim, such as their name, phone number, or address. This information can make the caller appear more trustworthy.

Criminals may also use official logos, uniforms, documents, and legal terminology to create an illusion of authority.

Another important tactic is preventing the victim from thinking clearly.

The caller may say that the victim has only a few minutes to respond or that refusing to cooperate will result in immediate arrest.

Under this pressure, people may transfer money without checking the facts.

Common Warning Signs of a Digital Arrest Scam

Knowing the warning signs can help you identify a fraudulent call before it becomes a financial disaster.

Be cautious if someone:

  • Calls unexpectedly and claims to be a police officer.
  • Accuses you of a crime without providing verifiable information.
  • Threatens immediate arrest through a phone or video call.
  • Orders you to remain on a video call for hours.
  • Tells you not to speak to your family or friends.
  • Demands money to close a criminal investigation.
  • Requests your OTP, PIN, banking password, or card details.
  • Asks you to install an unfamiliar application.
  • Pressures you to transfer money immediately.
  • Uses fear and secrecy to stop you from verifying the claim.

Even if the caller knows your personal details, do not assume that the call is genuine.

What Should You Do If You Receive a Digital Arrest Call?

The first thing to remember is simple: do not panic.

Receiving a threatening call does not mean that you have committed a crime or that you are under a genuine digital arrest.

If someone claims to be a police officer or government official, take the following steps.

1. End the Suspicious Call

You do not have to remain on a video call with someone who is threatening or intimidating you.

Disconnect the call and avoid responding to further demands.

2. Verify the Information Independently

If the caller claims to represent a government agency, contact that agency using the official contact details available on its website.

Do not call back using a number provided by the suspected scammer.

3. Never Share Sensitive Financial Details

Do not reveal your OTP, ATM PIN, internet banking password, card details, or other confidential information.

Also, avoid installing unknown applications or giving strangers access to your phone or computer.

4. Speak to Someone You Trust

Tell a family member, friend, or trusted person what happened.

Scammers often try to isolate their victims. Discussing the situation with someone else can help you identify suspicious claims and avoid making an impulsive payment.

5. Preserve the Evidence

Keep screenshots, phone numbers, messages, payment receipts, and other relevant details.

This information may help the bank, police, or cybercrime authorities investigate the incident.

How to Report a Digital Arrest Scam in India

If you receive a suspicious call or become a victim of a digital arrest scam, report it promptly.

For financial cyber fraud in India, you can contact the National Cyber Crime Helpline at 1930.

You can also file a complaint through the official National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal:

cybercrime.gov.in

If you have already transferred money, contact your bank immediately and explain that you may have been defrauded. Provide the transaction details and follow the bank's instructions.

Quick reporting may help authorities and financial institutions take action. However, recovering the money is not guaranteed, so it is important to report the fraud as soon as possible.

You can also approach your local police station if necessary.

How Can You Protect Yourself From Digital Arrest Scams?

Staying alert is one of the best ways to protect yourself from online fraud.

Remember these important precautions:

  • Do not trust unexpected calls claiming that you are involved in a criminal case.
  • Never transfer money to avoid arrest.
  • Verify the identity of anyone claiming to be a police officer.
  • Do not share personal or financial information with strangers.
  • Be careful when opening unknown links or downloading unfamiliar applications.
  • Talk to family members if you receive a threatening call.
  • Report suspicious activity to the cybercrime authorities.

It is also useful to educate older family members, who may be unfamiliar with online scams and more vulnerable to pressure from fraudsters.

Final Thoughts

Digital arrest scams show how cybercriminals can use fear and technology to steal money from innocent people. By pretending to be police officers or government officials, scammers create a false sense of urgency and authority.

But, no law enforcement agency can arrest you through a video call or demand money to make a criminal investigation disappear.

If you receive such a call, stay calm, disconnect, verify the information, and report the incident if necessary.

A threatening phone call is not proof of a crime. Taking a moment to think before acting could save you and your family from a major financial loss.
