Drupal 6 and 7 Websites: What Drupal 12 Means for Your Business Upgrade Plans

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Nov 17, 2026 10:08 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 17 November 2026
Drupal 6 and 7 Websites: What Drupal 12 Means for Your Business Upgrade Plans

A business website may continue working for years without requiring major changes. Customers can visit pages, read information, submit enquiries and access services. From the outside, everything may appear normal. However, the technology supporting a website also evolves, and businesses that continue using older platforms may eventually face challenges when they need improvements, maintenance or new features.

Drupal's latest announcement gives businesses another reason to review their existing websites. On November 12, 2026, Drupal confirmed that its upcoming Drupal 12 release will remove the migration support currently used for moving websites directly from Drupal 6 and Drupal 7. The project has recommended that organisations still using these versions complete their migration to Drupal 11 by mid-2028.

This does not mean older websites will suddenly stop functioning. However, it highlights why businesses should not treat website upgrades as something that can be postponed indefinitely.

For organisations that depend on their websites for customer communication, publishing, enquiries or daily operations, planning an upgrade early can help avoid unnecessary complications in the future.

Key Takeaways

  • Drupal 12 will remove the existing migration tools for Drupal 6 and Drupal 7 websites.
  • Businesses using these older versions should plan to migrate to Drupal 11 before upgrading to Drupal 12.
  • Drupal recommends completing the migration by mid-2028.
  • An older website may need more than a version upgrade, particularly if it has customised features or outdated functionality.
  • Reviewing the existing website early can help businesses understand the cost, effort and practical options involved.

Is Your Existing Drupal Website Becoming a Business Concern?

One of the reasons businesses delay website upgrades is that there may not be an obvious problem with their existing website.

A company website may still receive visitors, display its services and generate customer enquiries. A publishing platform may continue to publish articles, while an organisation's portal may still serve its users.

But website requirements rarely remain the same forever.

A business that initially needed a simple online presence may now require better enquiry management, improved navigation, additional service sections or integration with other business applications. A publishing organisation may need more flexible content management and improved website performance.

When the existing website becomes difficult to maintain or extend, even relatively small improvements can require more time and effort.

This is where businesses should look beyond whether their websites are currently working. They should also consider whether their existing platforms can support their future requirements.

Drupal 6 and Drupal 7 users have an additional reason to review this question because of the upcoming changes to Drupal's migration support.

Website Upgrade or Complete Rebuilding: What Makes More Sense?

Not every older website needs to be rebuilt from scratch.

In some cases, upgrading an existing website may be a practical option. In others, the website may have accumulated outdated features, unnecessary content and complicated functionality that make a fresh development approach more suitable.

The right decision depends on the condition of the existing website and what the business wants to achieve.

For example, a company with a relatively simple website may only need to move its existing content and essential features to a newer platform.

On the other hand, an organisation operating a large website with customised services, multiple user roles and complicated business requirements may benefit from reviewing its overall website structure before beginning the migration.

This is not a new consideration for Drupal website owners. In an earlier article, Drupal 7 to Drupal 8/9 Audits, I discussed why moving an older Drupal website often involves more than transferring content. The same general principle remains relevant today: understanding the existing website before deciding how to migrate it is important.

Businesses should consider the following before making a decision:

  • Does the existing website still meet current business requirements?
  • Are there features that need improvement or replacement?
  • Is the website difficult or expensive to maintain?
  • Does the organisation need new functionality?
  • Can the existing content and website structure be retained?
  • Would rebuilding certain sections provide a better long-term result?

Making these decisions before development begins can help avoid unnecessary expenses and prevent the new website from carrying forward problems that already existed.

What Happens to Your Existing Content and Website Functionality?

For many businesses, the website represents years of investment.

It may contain hundreds or thousands of articles, service descriptions, customer resources, images and other important information. Some websites may also support enquiry forms, user accounts, internal processes and integrations with external services.

Naturally, businesses want to know what happens to these elements when they upgrade.

A website migration should account for the information and functionality that the organisation needs to retain. This includes reviewing existing pages, identifying important website features and deciding how they should work in the new environment.

The experience of people managing the website also deserves attention. A migration is a good opportunity to review whether content editors can easily create, update and organise information. I have previously covered some of these considerations in Improvements for Editor Experience in Drupal.

Search engine visibility is another important consideration. Existing website addresses may already receive visitors from search engines or external websites. Changing these addresses without proper planning can affect how visitors and search engines reach important pages.

For this reason, migration planning should include content checks, URL preservation, redirects where necessary and testing before the new website goes live.

The objective should not simply be to move an existing website to a newer version. It should be to preserve its business value while making it easier to maintain and improve.

How Much Should a Business Budget for a Drupal Upgrade?

Website migration costs vary considerably depending on the existing website.

A small business website with standard pages may require less work than a large publishing platform or business portal with customised functionality.

The number of pages alone does not determine the complexity. The condition of the website, its existing features, the amount of content that needs to be retained and the improvements expected after migration all influence the overall effort.

Businesses should therefore avoid relying on generic migration estimates without first understanding their existing websites.

A proper assessment can help identify what needs to be retained, what can be improved and what may need to be developed again.

It also provides an opportunity to plan the project in stages rather than treating the entire migration as one large expense.

For organisations working with limited budgets, this can make it easier to prioritise essential improvements while planning additional features for a later stage.

Why Businesses Should Not Wait Until an Upgrade Becomes Urgent

Drupal's recommended mid-2028 migration timeline gives older website owners a reason to start evaluating their options.

Although migration will remain possible after that period through an intermediate development environment, delaying the decision can leave businesses with fewer convenient options and less time to prepare.

A migration project may involve reviewing existing functionality, planning content movement, arranging development resources and testing the new website before launch.

These activities cannot always be completed quickly, particularly when the existing website supports important business operations.

Website performance and security should also be part of this review. An older website may need attention beyond its version number. For readers interested in these areas, my earlier articles on improving Drupal website performance and the Drupal application security checklist cover two important aspects of maintaining a website.

Starting early also gives organisations time to compare development approaches, understand estimated costs and choose a solution that fits their requirements.

The same principle applies to businesses using other older website platforms. Technology changes continuously, and reviewing a website's long-term requirements is a sensible part of managing a business's online presence.

Choosing the Right Drupal Development Partner

A website migration is an important business decision, so choosing the right development partner deserves attention.

Businesses should look beyond the cost of moving their websites. They should understand whether the development team can assess the existing website, identify potential challenges and recommend a realistic approach.

Clear communication is particularly important. The business should understand what will be retained, what will change, how long the project may take and what support will be available after launch.

It is also worth discussing ongoing maintenance and hosting. The hosting environment should be suitable for the website's size, traffic and operational requirements. Businesses planning this transition may also find What to Know Before You Choose a Drupal Hosting Platform useful.

A successful migration should not be the end of the relationship with the development team. Websites need regular attention as business requirements and technology continue to change.

Working with a development partner who understands both the website and the organisation's objectives can make the transition considerably easier to manage.
