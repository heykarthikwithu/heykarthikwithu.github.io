Gutthi Vankaya Recipe: How to Make Andhra-Style Stuffed Brinjal Curry at Home

Gutthi Vankaya Recipe: How to Make Andhra-Style Stuffed Brinjal Curry at Home

Gutthi Vankaya, also known as Gutti Vankaya, is a popular South Indian stuffed brinjal curry known for its rich flavour and spicy, slightly tangy taste. Small brinjals are carefully slit and filled with a freshly prepared spice mixture before being cooked slowly until they become tender and absorb the flavours of the masala.

This dish is particularly popular in Andhra and Telangana-style cooking, where brinjal is often combined with roasted spices, peanuts, coconut, tamarind and other ingredients to create a bold and aromatic curry.

The name itself describes the cooking method. “Vankaya” refers to brinjal or eggplant, while “gutti” refers to the stuffed preparation. Unlike a simple brinjal fry, Gutthi Vankaya takes a little more preparation, but the result is a delicious curry that works especially well with hot rice, dal, chapati or roti.

Here is a simple way to make Gutthi Vankaya – Stuffed Brinjal Curry at home.

Ingredients for Gutthi Vankaya

For the Brinjals

  • 8 to 10 small fresh brinjals
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons cooking oil
  • Salt as required
  • A little water

Choose small, tender brinjals that are reasonably similar in size. They are easier to stuff and cook more evenly.

For the Stuffing Masala

  • 2 tablespoons peanuts
  • 2 tablespoons grated dry or fresh coconut
  • 1 tablespoon sesame seeds
  • 1 tablespoon coriander seeds
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 4 to 6 dried red chillies
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 3 to 4 garlic cloves
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon red chilli powder, optional
  • 1 teaspoon tamarind paste or a small piece of tamarind
  • Salt as required
  • 1 teaspoon jaggery, optional

The combination of roasted peanuts, sesame seeds, coconut and spices gives the stuffing its characteristic nutty and aromatic flavour. Tamarind adds a pleasant sourness, while a little jaggery can balance the heat and tang.

How to Prepare Gutthi Vankaya

Step 1: Wash and Prepare the Brinjals

Wash the brinjals thoroughly and pat them dry.

Remove the green stem if required, but keep the brinjal intact. Make two deep cuts in each brinjal in the shape of a cross, starting from the bottom and stopping before reaching the stem.

The four sections should remain attached near the top so that the brinjal can be opened slightly and filled with the masala.

Place the prepared brinjals in a bowl of water with a little salt. This helps prevent them from darkening while you prepare the stuffing.

Step 2: Roast the Ingredients

Heat a pan over low to medium heat.

Add the peanuts and roast them until they become aromatic and lightly browned. Remove them from the pan and set aside.

In the same pan, roast the sesame seeds, coriander seeds, cumin seeds and dried red chillies separately or together, depending on the size of the pan. Be careful not to burn the spices.

Finally, lightly roast the coconut.

Allow the roasted ingredients to cool.

Roasting the ingredients is an important part of the recipe because it brings out their natural aroma and gives the stuffing a deeper flavour.

Step 3: Prepare the Stuffing

Add the roasted peanuts, sesame seeds, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, red chillies, coconut, chopped onion and garlic to a mixer grinder.

Add tamarind, salt, turmeric and, if you prefer, a small amount of jaggery.

Grind everything into a thick, slightly coarse masala. Avoid adding too much water. The stuffing should be thick enough to stay inside the brinjals.

Taste the masala and adjust the salt, chilli or tamarind according to your preference.

Step 4: Stuff the Brinjals

Remove the brinjals from the water and gently pat them dry.

Carefully open each brinjal without separating the sections completely.

Take a small amount of the prepared masala and fill the centre of each brinjal. Do not pack it too tightly, as the brinjal will soften while cooking.

Keep any leftover masala aside. It can be added to the curry while the brinjals are cooking.

Step 5: Cook the Stuffed Brinjals

Heat oil in a wide, heavy-bottomed pan.

Carefully place the stuffed brinjals in the pan. Arrange them in a single layer as much as possible.

Cover the pan and cook on low heat for a few minutes.

Turn the brinjals gently from time to time so that they cook evenly. Add a few tablespoons of water if the pan becomes too dry.

Keep the heat low and allow the brinjals to cook slowly. This helps them become tender while allowing the stuffing to cook properly.

Step 6: Add the Remaining Masala

Once the brinjals begin to soften, add the remaining stuffing masala to the pan.

Mix gently so that the brinjals do not break apart.

Add a little water depending on how thick you want the curry. Cover and cook again on low heat until the brinjals are completely tender and the oil begins to separate from the masala.

The finished curry should have soft brinjals coated with a thick, aromatic masala.

How to Serve Gutthi Vankaya

Gutthi Vankaya is best served hot.

It pairs particularly well with steamed rice and dal. A spoonful of ghee over hot rice can also complement the spicy and tangy flavours of the curry.

You can also serve it with:

  • Chapati
  • Roti
  • Jowar roti
  • Plain rice
  • Curd rice
  • Dal and rice

The thick stuffing makes this curry flavourful enough to be served as one of the main dishes in a traditional Indian meal.

Tips for Making Tasty Gutthi Vankaya

Choose small brinjals: Small, fresh brinjals are easier to stuff and generally cook more evenly.

Do not cut the brinjals completely: Keep the pieces attached near the stem so the stuffing stays inside.

Roast the spices carefully: Spices should be aromatic but not burnt. Burnt spices can make the entire curry taste bitter.

Keep the stuffing thick: A watery masala will be difficult to fill into the brinjals.

Cook on low heat: Slow cooking allows the brinjals to become tender and gives the masala enough time to develop its flavour.

Use oil according to your preference: Gutthi Vankaya traditionally benefits from a generous amount of oil because it helps cook the brinjals and masala. However, the quantity can be adjusted.

Balance the flavours: The ideal stuffing should have a balance of spicy, nutty, tangy and slightly sweet flavours. Adjust chilli, tamarind and jaggery according to your taste.

Can You Make Gutthi Vankaya Without Coconut?

Yes. Coconut can be reduced or left out if you prefer. Peanuts, sesame seeds and roasted spices can still provide plenty of flavour and texture.

If you want a stronger peanut flavour, you can slightly increase the quantity of roasted peanuts while keeping the masala thick.

Is Gutthi Vankaya Spicy?

The level of spiciness depends largely on the type and quantity of dried red chillies used in the stuffing. You can reduce the chillies for a milder curry or use a combination of varieties to get colour and flavour without making the dish excessively hot.

Why Does Gutthi Vankaya Taste Better After Resting?

Like many Indian curries, the flavours of Gutthi Vankaya can become more pronounced after the curry rests for a while. The cooked brinjal continues to absorb the masala, making the dish particularly flavourful when served with rice.

Conclusion

Gutthi Vankaya is a wonderful example of how a simple vegetable can be turned into a rich and flavourful Indian curry. The preparation involves a few extra steps compared with an everyday brinjal fry, but roasting the spices, preparing the stuffing and slowly cooking the brinjals creates a dish with plenty of flavour.

The combination of brinjal with peanuts, sesame seeds, coconut, spices and tamarind gives this stuffed brinjal curry its distinctive taste. You can also adjust the spice level and ingredients to suit your family's preferences.

Serve Gutthi Vankaya – Stuffed Brinjal Curry with hot rice and dal, chapati or roti for a satisfying homemade meal.
