Thepla with Curd and Pickle: A Traditional Gujarati Breakfast That Makes a Perfect Everyday Meal

By Reddamma D | Published: Nov 13, 2026 8:18 AM IST
By Reddamma D , 13 November 2026
Thepla with Curd and Pickle: A Traditional Gujarati Breakfast That Makes a Perfect Everyday Meal

Thepla with curd and pickle is one of those simple Indian food combinations that can turn an ordinary breakfast into a satisfying meal. Popular in Gujarati households, thepla is a soft, mildly spiced flatbread that is usually prepared using whole wheat flour, fresh fenugreek leaves and a few everyday spices. Served with a bowl of fresh curd and a little pickle, it offers a combination of flavours that is difficult to resist.

Whether you are looking for a filling breakfast, a convenient lunchbox option or something easy to carry while travelling, thepla is a practical choice. It does not require complicated ingredients, and the dough can be prepared in advance to make cooking easier on busy mornings.

If you enjoy traditional Indian breakfast combinations, you can also try Aloo Paratha with Homemade Curd, another comforting meal that pairs freshly prepared Indian flatbread with creamy curd.

Let's look at how to prepare delicious Gujarati thepla at home and serve it with curd and pickle.

What Makes Thepla with Curd and Pickle Special?

Thepla is particularly popular in Gujarat, where it is enjoyed for breakfast, lunch and even as a travel-friendly meal. Unlike plain chapatis, thepla contains spices and often includes fenugreek leaves, which give it a distinctive aroma and flavour.

The combination of thepla, curd and pickle brings together three different tastes. The thepla has a mildly spicy and savoury flavour, curd adds a cool and creamy element, while pickle provides a tangy and spicy kick.

Another reason people enjoy this combination is its convenience. Thepla can be prepared in batches, stored for later consumption and packed into lunchboxes. It is equally suitable for a relaxed weekend breakfast or a quick weekday meal.

Gujarati cuisine has several other popular dishes that are worth exploring. For instance, Dhokla is another well-known Gujarati speciality, prepared using a fermented batter and steamed to create a soft, spongy snack.

Ingredients Required to Make Thepla

The ingredients are easily available in most Indian kitchens. Fresh methi leaves are commonly used, although dried fenugreek leaves can also be used when fresh leaves are unavailable.

For the thepla:

  • 2 cups whole wheat flour
  • 1 cup fresh methi (fenugreek) leaves, finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons curd
  • 1 teaspoon ginger-green chilli paste
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • ½ teaspoon red chilli powder
  • ½ teaspoon cumin powder
  • ½ teaspoon coriander powder
  • ½ teaspoon ajwain (carom seeds)
  • A pinch of hing (asafoetida)
  • Salt as required
  • 1 tablespoon oil for the dough
  • Water as required
  • Oil or ghee for roasting

For serving:

  • 1 cup fresh plain curd
  • Your favourite Indian pickle
  • A little chopped coriander, optional

How to Prepare Gujarati Thepla at Home

Preparing thepla is relatively simple. The important thing is to make a soft dough and roast each flatbread properly so that it remains flavourful and does not become too dry.

Step 1: Prepare the Methi Leaves

Wash the fresh methi leaves thoroughly under running water to remove dirt and impurities. Separate the leaves from the thicker stems and chop them finely.

If the leaves taste particularly bitter, you can sprinkle a little salt over them and leave them aside for a few minutes. Gently squeeze out the excess moisture before adding them to the flour.

Step 2: Prepare the Dough

Take a large mixing bowl and add whole wheat flour, chopped methi leaves, curd, ginger-green chilli paste, turmeric powder, red chilli powder, cumin powder, coriander powder, ajwain, hing and salt.

Add one tablespoon of oil and mix everything well.

Gradually add water and knead the mixture into a soft, smooth dough. Avoid adding too much water at once because the methi leaves and curd also contribute moisture.

Once the dough is ready, cover it with a clean cloth or plate and allow it to rest for approximately 15–20 minutes.

Step 3: Roll the Theplas

Divide the prepared dough into equal-sized portions and roll each portion between your palms to form small balls.

Dust a rolling surface with a little wheat flour. Place one dough ball on it and roll gently into a thin, round flatbread.

Try to maintain an even thickness so that the thepla cooks properly on both sides.

Repeat the process with the remaining dough portions.

Step 4: Cook the Theplas

Heat a tawa or flat pan over medium heat. Place one rolled thepla on the hot tawa.

Cook until small bubbles begin to appear on the surface. Flip it and apply a little oil or ghee around the edges.

Turn it again and gently press with a spatula to help it cook evenly. Continue flipping until both sides develop light golden-brown spots.

Remove the cooked thepla and place it on a plate. Repeat the same process with the remaining portions.

Your homemade Gujarati methi thepla is now ready to serve.

How to Serve Thepla with Curd and Pickle

The traditional way to enjoy thepla is to serve it warm with fresh curd and a small portion of Indian pickle.

Place two or three theplas on a serving plate. Add a small bowl of plain curd on one side and your preferred pickle on the other.

You can choose mango pickle, lemon pickle or chilli pickle depending on your taste. A little homemade mango pickle works particularly well because its tangy flavour complements the mild spices in the thepla.

If you prefer a slightly richer meal, you can add a small amount of homemade white butter or ghee.

For those who enjoy a refreshing side dish, sliced cucumber or a simple onion salad can also accompany the meal.

Curd is also used in several other Indian recipes. If you enjoy its creamy texture and mild flavour, you can explore How to Prepare Curd Rice, a popular South Indian comfort food made with cooked rice and fresh curd.

Tips for Making Soft and Flavourful Theplas

A few small adjustments can make a noticeable difference to the texture and taste of homemade thepla.

  • Use fresh methi leaves: Fresh fenugreek leaves provide a better aroma and flavour. Make sure they are washed and finely chopped.
  • Prepare a soft dough: A very stiff dough can result in hard theplas. Add water gradually until the dough becomes soft and manageable.
  • Do not roll too thick: Theplas should be relatively thin so that they cook evenly without remaining raw inside.
  • Maintain medium heat: Cooking on very high heat can burn the outside before the inside is properly cooked.
  • Use curd in the dough: A little curd helps bring the ingredients together and contributes to a softer texture.
  • Avoid excessive dry flour: Use only as much flour as needed while rolling. Too much dry flour can make the thepla taste dry.
  • Store properly: Allow the theplas to cool before stacking them in an airtight container to reduce excess moisture.

Can You Prepare Thepla Without Fresh Methi Leaves?

Yes. Although fresh methi leaves are commonly used, you can prepare thepla even when they are not available.

Kasuri methi, or dried fenugreek leaves, is a convenient alternative. Crush a small quantity between your palms before adding it to the flour. This helps release its aroma.

You can also prepare plain masala thepla using wheat flour, curd, turmeric, chilli powder, cumin and coriander powder. The result will have a slightly different flavour but still pairs wonderfully with curd and pickle.

Is Thepla a Good Breakfast or Travel Meal?

Thepla is a convenient option for people who want something filling without spending too much time in the kitchen every morning. It can be prepared in advance and packed for school, office or road trips.

When packed properly, plain theplas can remain suitable for consumption for a reasonable period, depending on the ingredients, preparation and storage conditions. If you are carrying curd, keep it separately in an insulated container with adequate cooling. Pickle can be packed in a small leak-proof container.

For longer journeys or warm weather, avoid leaving thepla with curd at room temperature for extended periods.

If you are planning a more elaborate weekend breakfast, you can also explore Chole Bhature, a popular Punjabi combination of spicy chickpea curry and fluffy fried bread.

Conclusion

Thepla with curd and pickle is a simple yet satisfying combination that reflects the everyday food traditions of Gujarat. With whole wheat flour, fresh methi leaves and a handful of common spices, you can prepare a delicious meal without relying on complicated cooking techniques.

The soft, mildly spiced thepla, creamy curd and tangy pickle complement one another beautifully. Whether you serve it for breakfast, pack it for lunch or prepare it for a family outing, this traditional Gujarati recipe is worth adding to your regular meal routine.

If you enjoy homemade Indian food that is practical, flavourful and easy to prepare, Gujarati thepla with curd and pickle is a recipe you can keep coming back to.
