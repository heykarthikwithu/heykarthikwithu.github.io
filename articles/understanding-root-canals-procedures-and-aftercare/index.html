Understanding Root Canals: Procedures and Aftercare

By Jyothi Karthik D | Published: Sep 10, 2026 8:57 AM IST
By Jyothi Karthik D , 10 September 2026
Understanding Root Canals: Procedures and Aftercare

A root canal treatment is one of the most commonly performed procedures to save a natural tooth that has become infected or severely damaged. Yet, the words “root canal” can still make many patients anxious. In my practice, I often meet patients who are worried that the treatment will be painful or that removing the tooth might be a better option.

The good news is that modern root canal treatment is a predictable procedure designed to remove infection, relieve pain, and preserve your natural tooth. With appropriate diagnosis, anesthesia, cleaning, sealing, and final restoration, a treated tooth can continue to function normally for many years.

What Is a Root Canal Treatment?

Inside every tooth is a soft tissue called the dental pulp, which contains nerves, blood vessels, and connective tissue. Deep decay, cracks, trauma, or repeated dental procedures can damage this tissue and allow bacteria to cause inflammation or infection.

A root canal treatment, also known as endodontic treatment, involves removing the infected or inflamed pulp, carefully cleaning and disinfecting the root canals, and then filling and sealing them to help prevent reinfection.

The objective is not simply to remove the nerve. The main goal is to eliminate infection while preserving the natural tooth.

Why Might You Need a Root Canal?

A root canal may be recommended when the pulp inside your tooth is irreversibly inflamed or infected.

Some common causes include:

  • Deep dental decay
  • A cracked or fractured tooth
  • Dental trauma or injury
  • Repeated dental procedures on the same tooth
  • Leakage around an existing filling or crown
  • Previous damage to the pulp

Patients may experience symptoms such as persistent toothache, sensitivity to hot or cold, pain while biting or chewing, swelling around the tooth, or a recurring gum abscess. However, an infected tooth does not always cause obvious pain, which is why regular dental examinations are important.

What Happens During a Root Canal Procedure?

I always encourage my patients to understand the procedure before treatment because knowing what to expect can make the experience much more comfortable.

1. Examination and Diagnosis

First, your dentist examines the tooth and assesses your symptoms. Dental X-rays or other diagnostic imaging may be used to evaluate the roots, surrounding bone, and extent of the infection.

An accurate diagnosis is essential because tooth pain can have several different causes, and not every painful tooth requires a root canal.

2. Local Anesthesia

The affected area is carefully numbed using local anesthesia. You remain awake during the procedure, but the goal is to ensure you are comfortable throughout treatment. Modern root canal treatment is generally performed comfortably with appropriate anesthesia.

3. Removing the Infected Pulp

A small opening is made in the tooth to access the pulp chamber and root canals. The infected or inflamed pulp tissue is then removed.

4. Cleaning and Disinfecting the Canals

The root canals are carefully cleaned, shaped, and disinfected. This is an important part of root canal treatment because bacteria and infected tissue need to be removed from the canal system before it can be sealed.

5. Filling and Sealing the Tooth

Once the canals have been cleaned and prepared, they are filled with a suitable material and sealed. This helps prevent bacteria from entering the canal system again.

6. Restoring the Tooth

Root canal treatment addresses the infection inside the tooth, but the tooth may still require a suitable final restoration.

Depending on the amount of tooth structure remaining and the tooth's location, your dentist may recommend a filling or a dental crown. A crown can provide additional protection to a weakened tooth, particularly when significant tooth structure has been lost.

Is a Root Canal Painful?

This is probably the question I hear most frequently from patients.

“Doctor, will the root canal hurt?”

With modern anesthesia and dental techniques, most patients are comfortable during root canal treatment. In fact, the procedure is intended to treat the source of dental pain rather than create it.

After treatment, it is normal for the tooth or surrounding area to feel mildly tender for a few days. This is particularly possible when there was significant inflammation or infection before treatment.

If you experience severe or persistent pain, significant swelling, or symptoms that are getting worse rather than better, you should contact your dentist for an assessment.

Root Canal Aftercare: What Should You Do?

Good root canal aftercare is important for protecting your treated tooth and allowing the surrounding tissues to settle.

Avoid Chewing Hard Foods Initially

If you have a temporary filling or the tooth has not yet received its permanent restoration, avoid chewing hard or sticky foods with the treated tooth. A tooth that has lost substantial tooth structure may be more vulnerable to fracture.

Maintain Good Oral Hygiene

You can generally continue brushing and cleaning between your teeth as advised by your dentist. Good oral hygiene helps protect both the treated tooth and the surrounding teeth and gums.

Follow Your Dentist's Medication Instructions

If medication is recommended for discomfort, take it according to your dentist's instructions and only if it is appropriate for your individual health circumstances.

Do Not Delay the Final Restoration

One of the most important parts of root canal aftercare is completing the recommended permanent restoration.

A temporary filling is not intended to provide the same long-term protection as a definitive restoration. Your dentist will advise you whether a filling, crown, or another restoration is appropriate.

How Long Does Root Canal Recovery Take?

Most patients can return to their usual activities relatively quickly after root canal treatment. Some tenderness around the treated tooth can persist for a few days and should gradually improve.

The exact recovery experience varies depending on the tooth, the extent of infection, the complexity of the root canal anatomy, and the type of restoration required.

If your symptoms become worse, swelling develops, your bite feels uncomfortable or uneven, or your previous symptoms return, contact your dentist promptly.

Can a Root Canal Save Your Natural Tooth?

Yes. One of the greatest advantages of root canal treatment is that it can allow you to keep your natural tooth.

When a tooth can be predictably treated and restored, preserving it can help maintain normal chewing function and the natural arrangement of your teeth. The American Association of Endodontists notes that properly treated teeth can often last for many years and, with appropriate care, potentially a lifetime.

However, every case is different. Some teeth may be too severely fractured or damaged to save. Your dentist will assess the condition of the tooth and discuss the available treatment options with you.

What Happens If a Root Canal Is Not Treated?

If an infected pulp is left untreated, the infection may progress and potentially result in an abscess or damage to the tissues surrounding the tooth. In some situations, the tooth may eventually require extraction.

This is why I advise my patients not to ignore persistent tooth pain, swelling, or prolonged sensitivity.

Pain may temporarily disappear even when an underlying dental problem remains. The absence of pain does not necessarily mean that an infection has resolved.

Can a Root Canal Fail?

Root canal treatment has a high level of predictability, but no dental treatment can guarantee success in every case.

A previously treated tooth can occasionally develop new problems months or even years later. Possible reasons include complex or untreated canal anatomy, new decay, leakage around a restoration, or a fracture. Depending on the situation, retreatment or endodontic surgery may sometimes provide an opportunity to save the tooth.

This is another reason why regular dental check-ups remain important even after successful root canal treatment.

How Can You Prevent the Need for a Root Canal?

Not every root canal problem can be prevented, particularly when dental trauma is involved. However, maintaining good oral health can reduce the risk of deep decay and other problems that can affect the dental pulp.

I recommend:

  • Brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste
  • Cleaning between your teeth every day
  • Limiting frequent sugary snacks and drinks
  • Attending regular dental examinations
  • Treating cavities at an early stage
  • Wearing an appropriate mouthguard during contact sports
  • Seeking dental advice promptly after a dental injury

Early treatment can often prevent a small dental problem from becoming a much more complicated one.

How long does a root canal take?

The duration depends on the tooth and the complexity of the case. Some root canals can be completed in one or two appointments, while others may require additional visits. Your dentist will explain the expected treatment schedule after examining your tooth.

Can I eat after a root canal?

It is generally best to wait until the local anesthesia has worn off before eating so that you do not accidentally bite your cheek, tongue, or lip. If you have a temporary restoration, your dentist may also advise you to avoid chewing hard foods on that tooth.

Do all root canal-treated teeth need a crown?

Not necessarily. The appropriate restoration depends on the tooth's location, remaining tooth structure, and individual clinical circumstances. Your dentist will recommend the restoration that provides appropriate protection and function.

What should I do if my tooth hurts after a root canal?

Mild tenderness can occur for a few days after treatment. Follow your dentist's aftercare instructions. If pain is severe, persistent, associated with swelling, or your original symptoms return, contact your dentist for an evaluation.

Is extraction better than root canal treatment?

Not always. When a natural tooth can be predictably saved, root canal treatment can preserve the tooth and its function. However, the best option depends on the condition of the tooth, surrounding tissues, and your overall dental health. Your dentist can discuss the benefits and limitations of each option with you.

Final Thoughts From Dr. Jyothi Karthik D

A root canal should not be something you fear. When a tooth has an infected or irreversibly damaged pulp, root canal treatment can provide an opportunity to relieve symptoms and preserve your natural tooth.

My advice to patients is simple: do not ignore persistent tooth pain, sensitivity, swelling, or discomfort while chewing. The earlier we diagnose the problem, the more treatment options we may have.

Every tooth is different, so your treatment should always be based on a proper clinical examination and diagnosis. If you have been advised to undergo root canal treatment, ask your dentist about the condition of your tooth, the treatment plan, the expected restoration, and how you can care for it afterward.

Note: This article is intended for general patient education and does not replace an individual dental examination or professional diagnosis.
