Inside the Bermuda Triangle: A Century of Missing Ships, Vanished Aircraft and Unanswered Questions

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Nov 4, 2026 8:03 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 4 November 2026
Inside the Bermuda Triangle: A Century of Missing Ships, Vanished Aircraft and Unanswered Questions

There are places in the world that become famous because of their natural beauty, historical importance or scientific significance. And then there are places that become famous because people cannot explain what happens there.

The Bermuda Triangle belongs to the third category.

For decades, stories of ships disappearing without sending distress signals, aircraft vanishing during routine flights and experienced sailors losing their sense of direction have surrounded this part of the Atlantic Ocean.

Some believe the region is connected to supernatural forces, extraterrestrial activity or mysterious underwater phenomena. Others argue that the incidents are the result of extreme weather, navigational errors, powerful ocean currents and human mistakes.

What makes the subject particularly interesting is that several historical disappearances have never been conclusively explained.

The mystery has survived for generations. Even in 2026, people continue to ask the same question: Is the Bermuda Triangle a genuine unsolved mystery, or is it a legend created by fear, coincidence and sensational storytelling?

To understand the answer, it is important to look at the history of the Bermuda Triangle, starting long before the name became famous.

What Is the Bermuda Triangle and Where Is It Located?

The Bermuda Triangle is a loosely defined region in the western North Atlantic Ocean. It is generally described as an area connecting three locations:

  • Miami, Florida, in the United States.
  • Bermuda, an island territory in the North Atlantic.
  • Puerto Rico in the Caribbean region.

The area is also popularly known as the Devil's Triangle.

However, there is an interesting detail that many people do not know. The Bermuda Triangle does not have officially recognised geographical boundaries.

The United States Board on Geographic Names does not recognise it as an official geographical name. There is no universally accepted map establishing its exact size.

Depending on the author or publication, the triangle has been described as covering approximately 500,000 square miles or even larger areas.

The region is also an important route for international shipping, commercial aviation and recreational boating.

Thousands of vessels and aircraft travel through these waters. Yet, because of several unusual historical incidents, this region gradually developed a reputation as one of the world's most mysterious places.

The Early Mystery: Did It Begin With Christopher Columbus?

Long before the expression Bermuda Triangle existed, sailors had reported unusual experiences while travelling through the Atlantic.

One of the earliest frequently mentioned accounts comes from Christopher Columbus's first voyage in 1492.

During his journey towards the Americas, Columbus reportedly recorded unusual compass readings. His crew also observed a strange light in the distance on one occasion.

Another account described what appeared to be a fireball falling into the sea.

These observations have often been included in discussions about the Bermuda Triangle.

However, there is an important distinction. Columbus did not identify a mysterious triangular region, nor did he report a ship disappearing because of supernatural activity.

His observations were later incorporated into the broader Bermuda Triangle legend.

At the time, navigation was far less advanced than it is today. Sailors depended on compasses, stars, maps and their understanding of ocean conditions. Unusual weather, unfamiliar waters and navigational uncertainty could easily create frightening experiences.

Nevertheless, these historical accounts helped establish the idea that something unusual might be happening in the western Atlantic.

1918: The Disappearance of USS Cyclops

One of the most important incidents associated with the Bermuda Triangle occurred in March 1918.

The USS Cyclops, a United States Navy cargo ship, disappeared after leaving Barbados while travelling towards Baltimore.

The ship was approximately 542 feet long and carried a large cargo of manganese ore. There were 306 people aboard, including crew members and passengers.

The vessel was an important part of the American naval transportation system during World War I.

After stopping at Barbados on March 4, 1918, the ship continued its journey. It never reached its destination.

No confirmed distress signal was received, and extensive search operations failed to locate the vessel or its occupants.

The disappearance created considerable concern because the USS Cyclops was a large ship. It was difficult for people to understand how such a vessel could vanish without leaving behind confirmed evidence.

Several explanations were considered, including severe weather, structural problems, difficulties associated with its heavy cargo and possible wartime attacks.

However, none was conclusively established. There is no confirmed evidence that an enemy submarine sank the ship.

More than a century later, the fate of the USS Cyclops remains unknown.

The incident became one of the most powerful examples used by writers discussing the Bermuda Triangle.

1945: Flight 19 – The Incident That Made the Mystery Famous

If one incident transformed the Bermuda Triangle into a worldwide mystery, it was the disappearance of Flight 19 on December 5, 1945.

Five United States Navy TBM Avenger torpedo bombers departed from Fort Lauderdale, Florida, for a routine training exercise.

The group consisted of 14 crew members, and the flight was led by Lieutenant Charles C. Taylor.

Initially, everything appeared normal. The aircraft completed their scheduled practice exercise.

However, problems began when the pilots attempted to navigate towards their next destination.

Radio communications indicated that the flight leader was confused about the aircraft's position. He reportedly could not identify the coastline and was uncertain about the direction in which the aircraft were travelling.

The pilots became increasingly concerned as their fuel supplies decreased.

Attempts were made to establish their location through radio communication, but the situation did not improve.

Eventually, communication with the five aircraft was lost.

The United States Navy launched a major search operation. However, another tragedy followed.

A Martin PBM Mariner rescue aircraft carrying 13 people also disappeared shortly after taking off to search for Flight 19.

In total, 27 people aboard six aircraft were lost.

Despite an extensive search covering more than 250,000 square miles of ocean and surrounding waters, no confirmed wreckage from the missing aircraft was recovered.

The disappearance of Flight 19 became one of the most discussed aviation mysteries in history.

The incident also generated several theories, including compass problems, navigational confusion, unexpected weather conditions and supernatural interference.

But the Navy's investigation could not establish a definitive explanation for the loss of the five Avengers.

Even today, the exact fate of the aircraft and their crews remains unknown.

The Missing Rescue Aircraft: Why Was the PBM Mariner Called a Flying Gas Tank?

There was another unusual detail about the rescue operation.

The Martin PBM Mariner was a large flying boat with a considerable fuel capacity. It had also earned the nickname "the flying gas tank" because of its tendency to accumulate gasoline vapours in certain parts of the aircraft and its associated fire risks.

The rescue aircraft disappeared shortly after taking off.

A merchant ship in the area reported seeing a large burst of flames, followed by an oil slick. The Navy's historical account considers an in-flight fire a likely explanation for the Mariner's loss, although the precise cause was never established.

This detail is important because the disappearance of the rescue aircraft has sometimes been presented as another unexplained supernatural event.

The available evidence, however, points towards a possible aviation accident.

The loss of the PBM Mariner remains a tragedy, but it should be considered separately from the unresolved fate of Flight 19.

1948 and 1949: Passenger Aircraft That Disappeared

The mystery did not end with Flight 19.

In January 1948, a British South American Airways passenger aircraft named Star Tiger disappeared while travelling from the Azores towards Bermuda.

The aircraft was carrying 31 people.

It failed to reach its destination, and no confirmed wreckage was recovered.

In January 1949, another aircraft operated by the same airline, Star Ariel, disappeared while flying from Bermuda towards Kingston, Jamaica.

There were 20 people aboard.

The aircraft had maintained communication during its journey, but it eventually disappeared without a confirmed explanation.

These incidents attracted further attention because they involved commercial passenger aircraft rather than military training missions.

The disappearances strengthened the belief that something unusual was happening in the region.

However, aviation investigators also recognised the difficulties of operating aircraft over large stretches of ocean, particularly during an era when navigation, weather forecasting and emergency communication systems were less advanced.

The investigations could not establish definitive causes for either disappearance.

1963: The Disappearance of Marine Sulphur Queen

Another famous incident occurred in February 1963.

The SS Marine Sulphur Queen, a tanker carrying molten sulphur, disappeared while travelling from Beaumont, Texas, towards Norfolk, Virginia.

There were 39 people aboard.

The vessel left Beaumont on February 2. It encountered difficult weather conditions during the voyage, and its last known communications were received on February 4.

Pieces of debris identified as belonging to the ship were later recovered. However, the vessel itself and its crew were never found.

The United States Coast Guard conducted an investigation into the disappearance.

Investigators considered several possible causes, including rough seas, structural problems and issues associated with the ship and its cargo.

The official inquiry concluded that the vessel and its entire crew must be presumed lost, but it could not establish the exact cause.

The disappearance became another incident frequently included in Bermuda Triangle books and documentaries.

However, it also demonstrated an important problem with the legend: not every disappearance was necessarily caused by an unusual geographical phenomenon. Some vessels had identifiable operational and safety risks.

1964: The Name Bermuda Triangle Becomes Famous

Interestingly, the Bermuda Triangle did not become internationally famous immediately after these incidents.

The expression became widely popular after American writer Vincent Gaddis published an article titled The Deadly Bermuda Triangle in Argosy magazine in 1964.

His article connected several mysterious disappearances and described the region as a place where ships and aircraft seemingly vanished under unusual circumstances.

This helped establish the Bermuda Triangle as a subject of public fascination.

In 1974, author Charles Berlitz published his bestselling book The Bermuda Triangle.

The book presented numerous disappearances and explored possible supernatural explanations.

It became extremely popular and helped spread the mystery internationally.

Stories involving alien encounters, underwater civilisations, unusual magnetic forces and unexplained energy fields became increasingly common.

However, the growing popularity of the subject also created a problem.

Some accounts were exaggerated, some incidents were incorrectly located, and others were included without sufficient evidence.

The Bermuda Triangle was no longer simply a collection of maritime and aviation incidents. It had become a worldwide mystery industry.

1975: The Investigation That Challenged the Bermuda Triangle Legend

A major challenge to the popular mystery came from journalist and researcher Larry Kusche.

In 1975, he published The Bermuda Triangle Mystery – Solved.

Rather than accepting the popular stories, Kusche investigated original reports, newspaper accounts, weather information and official records.

He discovered that some incidents had been inaccurately described.

In certain cases, vessels had disappeared outside the commonly described boundaries of the Bermuda Triangle. In others, the weather conditions were not as favourable as later stories suggested.

Some disappearances that had been presented as completely unexplained had possible ordinary explanations.

Kusche argued that the number of disappearances in the region was not unusually high compared with other heavily travelled areas of the ocean.

His work significantly challenged the popular belief that the Bermuda Triangle was a uniquely dangerous geographical location.

Nevertheless, his investigation did not solve every individual disappearance.

The USS Cyclops and Flight 19, for example, still lacked definitive explanations.

What Does Science Say About the Bermuda Triangle?

Over the years, scientists and researchers have examined several natural explanations for the incidents.

Although no single explanation accounts for every disappearance, the following factors could contribute to accidents in the region.

1. Hurricanes and Sudden Weather Changes

The western Atlantic experiences tropical storms and hurricanes.

Before modern weather forecasting, ships and aircraft had limited opportunities to identify dangerous weather conditions in advance.

Sudden storms, strong winds and enormous waves could create life-threatening situations.

The region also experiences rapidly changing weather conditions. A vessel or aircraft caught in severe weather could face serious difficulties before an effective rescue operation could be organised.

2. The Gulf Stream

The Gulf Stream is a powerful ocean current flowing through the western Atlantic.

It can move floating objects and wreckage over considerable distances.

As a result, debris from an accident may not remain close to the location where a ship or aircraft disappeared.

This can make search-and-rescue operations extremely difficult.

In the case of an aircraft or ship that sinks rapidly, locating its wreckage can be even more challenging.

3. Magnetic Compass Variations

Another popular theory involves unusual magnetic readings.

However, magnetic north and geographic north are not identical. The difference between them is known as magnetic declination.

Navigators must account for this difference when determining their direction.

There have also been discussions about locations where magnetic north and true north align, which could create confusion for navigators who do not understand the distinction.

But there is no established evidence of a mysterious magnetic force unique to the Bermuda Triangle that causes ships or aircraft to disappear.

4. Rogue Waves

Rogue waves are unusually large waves that can appear unexpectedly in the ocean.

They can pose a serious threat to ships, particularly under difficult sea conditions.

The Atlantic is capable of producing powerful waves, and a vessel caught in extreme conditions may suffer catastrophic damage.

However, there is no evidence that rogue waves occur unusually frequently in the Bermuda Triangle.

They are a natural ocean hazard, not proof of a mysterious force.

5. Methane Gas From the Ocean Floor

One particularly interesting theory involves methane gas trapped beneath the ocean floor.

According to this hypothesis, large quantities of methane could escape suddenly from underwater deposits.

If a sufficiently large release occurred beneath a ship, the gas bubbles could theoretically reduce the density of the surrounding water and affect buoyancy.

However, this explanation has significant limitations.

There is no convincing evidence connecting methane eruptions to the famous Bermuda Triangle disappearances. Nor has it been established that such releases caused the loss of the ships frequently mentioned in Bermuda Triangle stories.

It remains a hypothesis, not an established explanation.

What About Atlantis, Aliens and Time Portals?

Not every explanation associated with the Bermuda Triangle comes from science.

Some popular theories suggest that the region could be connected to the lost civilisation of Atlantis.

According to this idea, an advanced ancient civilisation may have left behind technology capable of producing unusual energy fields.

Others believe extraterrestrial visitors could be responsible for taking ships and aircraft into another dimension.

There are also theories involving time portals, underwater tunnels and mysterious forces that supposedly pull objects beneath the ocean.

These ideas have appeared in books, television programmes and popular culture.

However, there is no credible scientific evidence supporting these supernatural explanations.

They remain speculation rather than established facts.

The Bermuda Triangle in 2026: Has Science Finally Solved the Mystery?

The Bermuda Triangle continues to attract attention even in 2026.

Modern navigation systems, satellite tracking, weather forecasting and emergency communication have transformed maritime and aviation safety.

Ships can now use advanced positioning systems, while aircraft have sophisticated navigation and communication equipment.

These developments have made it much easier to track journeys and respond to emergencies.

Yet the historical disappearances continue to generate questions.

In July 2026, BBC Science Focus revisited the subject in an article titled How Science Solved the Bermuda Triangle Mystery.

The article examined how historical inaccuracies, exaggerated accounts, weather conditions and natural ocean processes helped create the legend.

It also highlighted the absence of evidence that the region experiences an unusually high number of accidents compared with other heavily travelled ocean areas.

The broader scientific position remains unchanged: there is no established evidence of supernatural forces or a special geographical phenomenon responsible for the disappearances.

The mystery has not completely disappeared, however.

The exact fate of certain historical vessels and aircraft, particularly the USS Cyclops and Flight 19, remains unknown.

That is an important distinction when discussing the Bermuda Triangle in 2026.

Science has challenged the idea of a supernatural triangle, but it has not recovered every missing vessel or aircraft or established the precise cause of every historical accident.

Is the Bermuda Triangle a Mystery or Just a Myth?

After examining its history, scientific explanations and famous disappearances, the answer is more complicated than simply choosing between science and superstition.

The Bermuda Triangle is not a scientifically established supernatural region.

There is no convincing evidence of aliens, time portals, mysterious energy fields or extraordinary magnetic forces causing ships and aircraft to disappear.

There is also no reliable evidence that the region experiences more disappearances than other similarly busy ocean areas.

At the same time, some historical incidents remain genuinely unresolved.

We still do not know exactly what happened to the USS Cyclops in 1918. We do not have a definitive account of the final moments of Flight 19 in 1945.

These are real historical tragedies, not fictional stories.

Perhaps the greatest mystery is not an invisible force hiding beneath the Atlantic Ocean. It is how a combination of genuine disappearances, incomplete investigations, sensational reporting and human fascination created a legend that survived for more than a century.

Even in 2026, the Bermuda Triangle remains a fascinating historical mystery, but the evidence points towards natural dangers and unanswered accidents rather than supernatural activity.

The ocean may eventually reveal more about some of these disappearances. Until then, the Bermuda Triangle will continue to occupy a unique place between history, science and human imagination.
