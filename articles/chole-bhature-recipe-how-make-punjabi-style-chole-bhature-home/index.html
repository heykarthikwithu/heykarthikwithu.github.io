Chole Bhature Recipe: How to Make Punjabi-Style Chole Bhature at Home

Chole Bhature Recipe: How to Make Punjabi-Style Chole Bhature at Home

Chole Bhature is one of those dishes that can turn an ordinary meal into something special. The combination of spicy chickpea curry, soft and fluffy fried bread, crunchy onions and a squeeze of lemon makes it a favourite among people who enjoy North Indian food. Although it is widely available in restaurants and street-food stalls, preparing Chole Bhature at home allows you to adjust the spice level, control the ingredients and enjoy it fresh from the kitchen.

This Punjabi-style Chole Bhature recipe explains how to prepare both parts of the dish without making the process unnecessarily complicated. The chole is cooked with onions, tomatoes and aromatic spices until the gravy becomes thick and flavourful. The bhature is made with maida and curd, then deep-fried until it puffs up and develops a light golden colour.

If you are planning a weekend breakfast, a filling lunch or a special family meal, this homemade Chole Bhature recipe is worth trying.

Chole Bhature Recipe: Preparation Details

  • Preparation time: 20 minutes, excluding chickpea soaking
  • Soaking time: 8 hours or overnight
  • Cooking time: 45–60 minutes
  • Bhature dough resting time: 1–2 hours
  • Servings: 3–4 people
  • Difficulty level: Moderate

Ingredients for Punjabi-Style Chole

  • 1 cup dried white chickpeas (kabuli chana)
  • 2 medium onions, finely chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped or pureed
  • 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 green chilli, slit
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • 1 teaspoon chole masala powder
  • ½ teaspoon red chilli powder
  • ¼ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • ½ teaspoon garam masala
  • 1 teaspoon amchur (dry mango powder), or to taste
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • Salt to taste
  • 2–3 tablespoons chopped coriander leaves
  • Water as required

Ingredients for Bhature

  • 2 cups maida (all-purpose flour)
  • ¼ cup fine sooji (semolina)
  • ½ cup thick curd
  • ½ teaspoon sugar
  • ½ teaspoon salt, or to taste
  • ¼ teaspoon baking powder
  • ¼ teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • Water as required
  • Oil for deep-frying

How to Make Chole Bhature at Home

Step 1: Soak and Cook the Chickpeas

Wash the chickpeas thoroughly and soak them in plenty of water for at least eight hours, preferably overnight. Chickpeas absorb water and expand during soaking, so use a sufficiently large bowl.

Drain the soaking water and transfer the chickpeas to a pressure cooker. Add fresh water, salt and the bay leaf. Pressure-cook until the chickpeas become completely soft. The cooking time depends on the chickpeas, soaking duration and cooker, so check that they can be easily pressed between your fingers.

Keep some of the cooking liquid aside. It can be added later to adjust the consistency of the chole.

Step 2: Prepare the Chole Masala

Heat oil in a thick-bottomed pan or kadai. Add the cumin seeds and allow them to release their aroma. Add the chopped onions and sauté until they turn golden brown.

Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook for another minute or two, stirring regularly. Mix in the chopped tomatoes or tomato puree and cook until they soften and the mixture thickens.

Now add the coriander powder, chole masala, red chilli powder and turmeric powder. Stir well and cook the mixture over a medium flame until the raw smell of the spices disappears.

Avoid adding too much water at this stage. Allowing the onion-tomato mixture to cook properly helps develop a richer flavour.

Step 3: Cook the Chickpeas in the Masala

Add the cooked chickpeas to the prepared masala and mix thoroughly. Pour in some reserved cooking liquid and add more water if necessary.

Simmer over a low flame for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally. Gently mash a few chickpeas against the side of the pan. This helps thicken the gravy naturally without requiring extra ingredients.

Add the garam masala, slit green chilli and amchur powder. Taste and adjust the salt, spice and tanginess. If the gravy is too thick, add a little hot water. If it is too thin, simmer for a few more minutes.

Finish with chopped coriander leaves. The chole should have a well-seasoned gravy that coats the chickpeas rather than a watery consistency.

Step 4: Prepare the Bhature Dough

While the chole simmers, prepare the bhature dough. Take the maida, sooji, sugar, salt, baking powder and baking soda in a large mixing bowl.

Add the curd and one tablespoon of oil. Mix everything together and gradually add small amounts of water, kneading until you obtain a smooth, soft dough. Do not pour in all the water at once, as the quantity required may vary depending on the flour and curd.

Cover the dough with a clean, damp cloth or keep it in a covered bowl. Allow it to rest for one to two hours. This resting period helps the dough become easier to roll and contributes to a softer texture.

Step 5: Roll the Bhature

Divide the rested dough into equal-sized balls. Lightly grease your hands and rolling surface with oil to prevent sticking.

Roll each ball into a round or oval shape, slightly thicker than a puri. Avoid rolling the dough too thin, as the bhature may not puff up properly.

Prepare the remaining portions before frying so that you can work comfortably and serve the bhature fresh.

Step 6: Deep-Fry Until Puffy

Heat oil in a deep kadai over medium-high heat. To check whether the oil is ready, drop in a tiny piece of dough. It should rise to the surface fairly quickly without immediately turning dark brown.

Carefully slide one rolled bhatura into the hot oil. Gently press the surface with a slotted spoon and spoon hot oil over it to encourage puffing. Once it expands, turn it over and fry the other side until lightly golden.

Remove the bhatura and place it on a plate lined with kitchen paper to drain excess oil. Repeat with the remaining dough portions.

Fry one bhatura at a time, maintaining a suitable oil temperature. If the oil is too cool, the bread may absorb excess oil; if it is too hot, the outside may brown before the inside cooks properly.

Step 7: Serve the Chole Bhature

Transfer the hot chole to serving bowls and place the freshly fried bhature alongside them. Add sliced onions, lemon wedges and green chillies, if desired.

A little pickle or mint chutney can provide an additional burst of flavour. Serve immediately while the bhature are still warm and puffed.

Tips for Making Better Chole Bhature

Soak the chickpeas properly: Overnight soaking helps the chickpeas cook more evenly. If they remain firm after pressure-cooking, continue cooking until tender.

Give the chole time to simmer: The masala and chickpeas need time to develop flavour together. Mashing a few chickpeas also helps create a thicker gravy.

Do not skip resting the dough: Resting helps the bhature dough relax and makes rolling easier. Follow the resting time rather than rushing directly to frying.

Maintain the right oil temperature: Properly heated oil is important for puffed bhature. Adjust the flame between batches if the oil becomes too hot.

Prepare the chole ahead: You can cook the chole earlier and reheat it before serving. Add a splash of water if the gravy thickens during storage. For the best texture, prepare and fry the bhature just before the meal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Chole Bhature be made without overnight soaking?

Dried chickpeas are best soaked overnight for even cooking. If you are short on time, a quick-soaking method may help, but the chickpeas must still be cooked until completely tender. Canned chickpeas are another convenient option; rinse and drain them before adding them to the masala.

Why do bhature fail to puff up?

Common reasons include dough that is too stiff, uneven rolling, insufficient resting time or oil at an unsuitable temperature. Roll the dough evenly and test the oil with a small piece before frying the first bhatura.

Can Chole Bhature be prepared in advance?

Yes. The chole can be prepared a day ahead and refrigerated in a covered container after cooling promptly. Reheat it thoroughly before serving. Bhature are best fried fresh because their texture changes as they cool.

What can be served with Chole Bhature?

Sliced onions, lemon wedges, green chillies, mango pickle and mint chutney are popular accompaniments. A glass of lassi can also complement this filling meal.

Enjoy Homemade Punjabi Chole Bhature

Making Chole Bhature at home involves a few separate steps, but each one is manageable with a little planning. The secret lies in properly cooked chickpeas, a well-developed masala, rested dough and freshly fried bhature.

Try this recipe when you want to prepare something special for your family or guests. Once you become comfortable with the method, you can adjust the spices and tanginess to suit your taste and make this classic North Indian dish your own.
