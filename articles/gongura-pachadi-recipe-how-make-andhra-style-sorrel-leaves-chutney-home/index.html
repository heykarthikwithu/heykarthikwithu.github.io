Gongura Pachadi Recipe: How to Make Andhra-Style Sorrel Leaves Chutney at Home

Gongura Pachadi is one of the most loved traditional recipes in Andhra Pradesh, known for its distinctive tangy taste, spicy flavour and simple preparation. Made with fresh gongura leaves, green or red chillies, garlic and a few basic spices, this Andhra-style chutney is a staple in many Telugu households. It pairs beautifully with hot steamed rice and a generous spoonful of ghee, making it a comforting dish for everyday meals.

Gongura, also known as sorrel leaves, is popular for its naturally sour flavour. Unlike many other leafy vegetables, gongura gives this chutney its signature tanginess without requiring tamarind or lemon juice in the traditional preparation. The combination of cooked leaves, roasted spices and garlic creates a flavourful pachadi that can easily become the highlight of a meal.

The best part about making Gongura Pachadi at home is that it does not require complicated ingredients or advanced cooking skills. With fresh leaves and a little patience, you can prepare an authentic Andhra gongura chutney that tastes delicious with rice, dosa, idli and other South Indian dishes.

What Is Gongura Pachadi?

Gongura Pachadi is a traditional Andhra chutney prepared using gongura leaves, which belong to the sorrel family. The leaves have a naturally sour taste, making them ideal for preparing spicy chutneys, pickles and other regional dishes.

In Andhra Pradesh, gongura is available in two commonly recognised varieties: green-stemmed gongura and red-stemmed gongura. Both varieties are used in traditional cooking, although their sourness and flavour can vary. The red-stemmed variety is often associated with a stronger, more pronounced tangy taste.

Every household has its own version of Gongura Pachadi. Some families prefer a simple preparation with green chillies, garlic and salt, while others add dried red chillies, cumin seeds, coriander seeds or roasted fenugreek seeds for additional flavour. The chutney may be ground into a coarse mixture or prepared with a slightly smoother texture, depending on personal preference.

Gongura Pachadi Recipe: Ingredients Required

The following ingredients make approximately four servings of homemade Andhra-style Gongura Pachadi.

  • Fresh gongura leaves – 2 large bunches, approximately 250–300 grams before cleaning
  • Green chillies – 6 to 8, depending on spice preference
  • Dried red chillies – 3 to 4
  • Garlic cloves – 8 to 10
  • Cumin seeds – 1 teaspoon
  • Coriander seeds – 1 teaspoon
  • Fenugreek seeds – ¼ teaspoon
  • Mustard seeds – ½ teaspoon
  • Turmeric powder – ¼ teaspoon, optional
  • Salt – as required
  • Oil – 3 tablespoons
  • Water – a small amount, only if needed during grinding

For the tempering:

  • Oil – 1 tablespoon
  • Mustard seeds – ½ teaspoon
  • Cumin seeds – ½ teaspoon
  • Dried red chilli – 1 or 2
  • Curry leaves – 8 to 10
  • Asafoetida – a small pinch, optional

Adjust the number of chillies according to your taste. Since gongura itself is sour, there is generally no need to add tamarind to this recipe.

Step 1: Clean and Prepare the Gongura Leaves

Separate the gongura leaves from the thick stems and discard any damaged or yellow leaves. Wash the leaves thoroughly under running water to remove dust, dirt and other impurities.

Drain the water completely and spread the leaves on a clean kitchen towel for a few minutes. Removing excess moisture helps the leaves cook properly without becoming unnecessarily watery.

Step 2: Cook the Gongura Leaves

Heat two tablespoons of oil in a heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat. Add the cleaned gongura leaves and stir gently.

The leaves will gradually wilt and release moisture as they cook. Continue stirring until they become soft and their volume reduces considerably. Cook until most of the released moisture evaporates and the leaves form a soft mixture.

This usually takes around 8–12 minutes, depending on the quantity and freshness of the leaves. Transfer the cooked gongura to a plate and allow it to cool.

Step 3: Roast the Chillies and Spices

In the same pan, add a little oil if required. Add the dried red chillies, cumin seeds, coriander seeds and fenugreek seeds.

Roast them over a low flame until they release their aroma. Be careful not to burn the spices, particularly the fenugreek seeds, as excessive roasting can make the chutney bitter.

Add the green chillies and roast them briefly until they soften slightly. Switch off the flame and let the ingredients cool.

Step 4: Grind the Chutney Base

Add the roasted chillies, cumin seeds, coriander seeds, fenugreek seeds, garlic and salt to a mixer grinder.

Grind the mixture into a coarse paste. You can add a teaspoon of water if absolutely necessary, but it is better to keep the mixture relatively thick.

Traditional Gongura Pachadi does not need to be completely smooth. A slightly coarse texture helps retain the character of the roasted spices and garlic.

Step 5: Add the Cooked Gongura Leaves

Add the cooled gongura leaves to the ground spice mixture. Pulse the mixer a few times until the leaves combine with the spices.

Taste the chutney and adjust the salt or chilli level if needed. Avoid grinding for too long if you prefer a traditional, coarse-style pachadi.

If you enjoy a rustic texture, you can also use a mortar and pestle to crush the cooked leaves and spices together.

Step 6: Prepare the Tempering

Heat one tablespoon of oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and allow them to splutter. Add cumin seeds, dried red chillies, curry leaves and a pinch of asafoetida, if using.

Fry the ingredients briefly until aromatic. Pour the tempering over the prepared Gongura Pachadi and mix well.

Your homemade Andhra-style Gongura Pachadi is now ready to serve.

Tips for Making Delicious Andhra Gongura Pachadi

Choose fresh leaves: Fresh, healthy gongura leaves provide the best flavour. Avoid leaves that appear yellow, damaged or wilted.

Balance the sourness: Gongura varieties differ in their natural sourness. If the leaves are particularly tangy, you can balance the flavour with a little more roasted chilli and garlic rather than adding unnecessary souring ingredients.

Roast the spices carefully: Cumin, coriander and fenugreek seeds should be roasted gently. Burnt spices can overpower the chutney and introduce bitterness.

Keep the texture slightly coarse: A traditional pachadi often has a rustic texture rather than the smooth consistency of a restaurant-style chutney.

Use oil for flavour: Oil helps bring the spices together and improves the overall taste. Groundnut oil or sesame oil can work well, depending on your preference.

Avoid adding too much water: Excess water can make the chutney runny and may shorten its storage life. Grind the ingredients using as little water as possible.

What to Eat with Gongura Pachadi

The most traditional way to enjoy Gongura Pachadi is with hot steamed rice and a spoonful of ghee. Mix a small portion of the chutney into the rice and serve it with a simple vegetable curry, papad or roasted vegetables.

It also pairs well with:

  • Idli and dosa for a flavourful South Indian breakfast.
  • Pesarattu, particularly if you enjoy bold, spicy chutneys.
  • Curd rice as a tangy accompaniment, served separately or in small quantities.
  • Dal rice and simple vegetable meals.
  • Chapati or roti when you want a spicy side dish.

Because the chutney has a strong sour and spicy flavour, start with a small serving and adjust the quantity to your taste.

How to Store Gongura Pachadi

Allow the pachadi to cool completely before transferring it to a clean, dry, airtight container. Refrigerate it promptly and always use a clean, dry spoon when serving.

For the best flavour and quality, consume homemade Gongura Pachadi within approximately 3–4 days when refrigerated at 4°C or below. Its actual storage life depends on the preparation, moisture content and handling. Do not assume that a layer of oil makes the chutney shelf-stable.

If you want to prepare a larger quantity, consider making smaller batches and freezing portions separately. Discard the chutney if you notice mould, an unusual smell or other signs of spoilage.

Is Gongura Pachadi Healthy?

Gongura leaves provide nutrients, including vitamins and minerals, and contribute dietary fibre to the diet. Preparing the leaves with garlic and spices adds flavour without requiring complicated sauces or packaged ingredients.

However, the nutritional value of the finished chutney depends on the ingredients and quantities used, particularly the amount of oil and salt. Gongura is also naturally rich in oxalates, so people who have been advised to limit dietary oxalates should follow their healthcare professional's guidance.

As with any strongly flavoured accompaniment, enjoying Gongura Pachadi in moderate portions alongside a varied diet is a practical approach.

Frequently Asked Questions About Gongura Pachadi

1. Can Gongura Pachadi be made without garlic?

Yes. You can prepare it without garlic by increasing the roasted spices slightly or adding a little extra cumin for flavour. The taste will differ from the traditional garlic-based version.

2. Can I use red-stemmed gongura for this recipe?

Yes. Both red-stemmed and green-stemmed gongura can be used. The final flavour depends on the variety, freshness and sourness of the leaves.

3. Why does my Gongura Pachadi taste bitter?

Bitterness may result from over-roasting fenugreek seeds or using burnt chillies and spices. Use a low flame and roast the spices only until fragrant.

4. Do I need to add tamarind to Gongura Pachadi?

No. Gongura naturally provides the sour flavour that defines this chutney. Tamarind is generally unnecessary in a traditional preparation.

5. How long does homemade Gongura Pachadi last?

When promptly refrigerated in a clean, airtight container at 4°C or below, it is best consumed within approximately 3–4 days. For longer storage, freeze it in small portions.

Conclusion

Gongura Pachadi is a wonderful example of Andhra Pradesh's rich culinary traditions, bringing together the natural sourness of gongura leaves, the heat of chillies and the aroma of roasted spices. Its simple ingredients and straightforward preparation make it an excellent choice for anyone who wants to explore authentic Telugu home cooking.

Whether you serve it with hot rice and ghee, pair it with dosa or enjoy it alongside a traditional South Indian meal, homemade Gongura Pachadi adds a distinctive flavour to the table. Try this easy Andhra-style Gongura Pachadi recipe at home and adjust the spices to create a version that suits your family's taste.
