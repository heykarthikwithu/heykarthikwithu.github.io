The Man Who Wanted Everything – A Moral Story About Greed, Wealth and Happiness

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Nov 7, 2026 8:35 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 7 November 2026
The Man Who Wanted Everything – A Moral Story About Greed, Wealth and Happiness

There was once a wealthy man named Raghav who lived in a beautiful mansion on the outskirts of a busy city. He owned several businesses, expensive cars, large properties, and everything that money could buy.

People in the town admired him. Some wished they could live his life, while others wondered how one person could accumulate so much wealth.

But Raghav was never satisfied.

Whenever he bought something expensive, he would enjoy it for a few days before looking for something even better. If his neighbour purchased a new car, Raghav wanted two. If another businessman built a bigger house, he immediately started planning an even grander one.

For Raghav, having enough was never an option. There was always something more to acquire.

His wife, Meera, often reminded him that they already had more than they needed.

"Why do you keep working so hard? We have a beautiful home, enough money for our children, and everything we could possibly need. Why can't we simply enjoy what we have?" she asked one evening.

Raghav barely looked up from his phone.

"Money gives us security, Meera. The more we have, the better our future will be. I don't want to stop when there is still so much to achieve."

Meera smiled sadly. She knew that no amount of explanation could change his mind.

When Money Became More Important Than Family

Raghav's business continued to grow. He started investing in hotels, purchasing farmland, and acquiring properties in different cities.

His bank balance increased every year, but his time with his family decreased.

His son, Arjun, often waited for him to return home so they could play cricket together. His daughter, Ananya, wanted him to attend her school performances. Meera simply wished they could sit together for dinner without business calls interrupting them.

But Raghav always had an excuse.

"I have an important meeting."

"I need to speak to an investor."

"This deal cannot wait."

Gradually, his children stopped asking him to spend time with them.

One evening, Arjun came to his father's office with a small drawing he had made at school.

"Papa, look! I drew our family," he said excitedly.

Raghav was busy signing documents.

"That's nice, son. Leave it on the table. I'll see it later."

Arjun stood there for a moment, hoping his father would look at the drawing. When he realised that Raghav was not paying attention, he quietly walked away.

The drawing remained untouched on the office table for several days.

Raghav was building a financial empire but unknowingly allowing his family to drift away.

The Endless Desire for More

Years passed, and Raghav became one of the richest businessmen in the region.

He purchased a luxurious farmhouse, added another expensive car to his collection, and even bought a holiday property abroad.

Yet, whenever he visited someone else's home or saw another person's success, he felt an uncomfortable desire to have more.

One day, his old friend Mahesh visited him.

Mahesh was not particularly wealthy. He lived in a modest house, ran a small business, and spent most evenings with his family.

As the two friends sat in Raghav's expensive garden, Mahesh looked around and said, "You have built an incredible life, Raghav. You have achieved things many people only dream about."

Raghav smiled proudly.

"Yes, but I am not finished yet. I am planning to acquire another company. Once that happens, I will be among the biggest business owners in the country."

Mahesh laughed gently.

"Tell me something, my friend. When will you decide that you have enough?"

Raghav looked surprised.

"Enough? Why should I stop earning when I have the ability to earn more?"

Mahesh took a moment before answering.

"There is nothing wrong with earning money or becoming successful. But if you spend your entire life collecting things, when will you find time to enjoy them?"

Raghav dismissed the question with a smile.

He believed Mahesh simply lacked ambition.

The Day Everything Changed

One afternoon, Raghav returned home earlier than usual. He was excited because he had just completed another major business acquisition.

He wanted to share the news with his family.

But when he entered the living room, the house felt unusually quiet.

Meera was sitting alone, reading a book.

"Where are the children?" he asked.

"Arjun has moved to another city for work. Ananya is studying abroad," she replied.

Raghav nodded.

"That's good. They should build successful careers."

Meera closed her book and looked directly at him.

"They are doing well, Raghav. But do you know what hurts? They no longer expect you to be part of their lives."

He frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"Arjun stopped calling because you were always too busy. Ananya stopped sharing her achievements because you never had time to listen. Even when we needed you, your business always came first."

Raghav became defensive.

"Everything I did was for this family! I worked day and night to give all of you a comfortable life."

Meera replied quietly, "We never asked you for more cars or bigger houses. We wanted your time, your attention, and your presence."

Her words stayed with him long after she left the room.

For the first time, Raghav began to wonder whether all his achievements had come at a price he had never considered.

A Mansion Full of Possessions but Empty of Happiness

A few weeks later, Raghav organised a grand birthday celebration at his mansion.

He invited business partners, influential people, and several wealthy acquaintances. The house was decorated beautifully, the food was excellent, and expensive gifts filled the tables.

But something was missing.

His children could not attend. Meera left early because she was tired of the endless business conversations. Most guests were interested in discussing investments, contracts, and opportunities.

Nobody seemed genuinely interested in Raghav himself.

After everyone left, he walked through the enormous house.

He looked at the expensive paintings, the imported furniture, the shining cars outside, and the trophies displayed in his office.

He had spent years collecting things that impressed other people.

Yet, at that moment, none of them could make him feel less lonely.

He picked up Arjun's old drawing from a drawer. It showed four people holding hands beneath a bright yellow sun.

Raghav stared at it for a long time.

He suddenly remembered how excited his son had been when he first showed him that drawing.

He had earned enough money to buy almost anything, but he could not buy back the moments he had missed.

Understanding the Meaning of True Wealth

The following morning, Raghav made a decision.

He called Arjun and Ananya, not to discuss business or ask about their achievements, but simply to ask how they were doing.

At first, the conversations were short and uncomfortable. His children were not used to receiving calls from him without a reason.

But Raghav did not give up.

He began visiting them, listening to their stories, and making time for family gatherings. He also started spending more time with Meera.

He did not abandon his business or give away all his possessions. Instead, he changed the way he looked at success.

He learned to appreciate what he already had.

He understood that earning money was important, but money should support life, not replace it.

Over time, his relationships slowly improved. His children began sharing their experiences again, and his home started feeling warmer.

One evening, while sitting with his family at the dinner table, Raghav looked around and smiled.

There were no business discussions, no urgent phone calls, and no expensive celebrations.

Just his wife and children, sharing stories and laughing together.

For the first time in many years, he felt genuinely rich.

He finally understood that the greatest wealth in life is not what we own, but the people who stand beside us.

Moral of the Story

Wealth without contentment cannot bring happiness.

Money can provide comfort, security, and opportunities, but it cannot replace love, friendship, family, or meaningful relationships.

There is nothing wrong with ambition or working towards financial success. However, when the desire for more becomes endless, we may lose sight of the things that make life worth living.

True happiness comes from appreciating what we have, spending time with the people we love, and understanding when enough is enough.

Remember, a person may own a mansion and still feel lonely, while someone living in a small home may enjoy a life filled with love and happiness.

Don't spend your entire life collecting things and forget to collect beautiful memories.
