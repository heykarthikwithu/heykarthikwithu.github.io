The Mango Tree and the Axe – A Moral Story About Kindness and Generosity

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Oct 31, 2026 8:47 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 31 October 2026
The Mango Tree and the Axe – A Moral Story About Kindness and Generosity

In a small village, there stood a large mango tree near the boundary of a farmer's field. The tree was on the farmer's property, but its branches stretched beyond the field, offering shade to travellers, farmers, children and anyone who needed a place to rest.

It had grown there for many years without much attention from the farmer. Every summer, it produced delicious yellow mangoes. Birds built their nests among its branches, squirrels played around its trunk, and villagers often gathered beneath it to escape the afternoon heat.

The tree never asked who deserved its shade or who deserved its fruit. It simply gave what it had.

People loved the mango tree, but not everyone appreciated its generosity.

The Man Who Wanted to Cut Down the Tree

One year, a farmer decided to expand his field. He looked at the mango tree standing near the boundary and thought it was taking up valuable space.

"If I cut down this tree, I can use the land to grow more crops," he said to himself.

The farmer knew the tree was old and healthy. He also knew that many villagers depended on it for shade and fruit. But he was more interested in the money he could earn from the additional land.

One morning, he picked up a sharp axe and walked towards the tree.

As he approached, a few villagers noticed him.

"Why are you carrying an axe?" one of them asked.

"I am going to cut down that mango tree," the farmer replied.

The villagers were surprised.

"Why would you destroy such a beautiful tree? It gives us shade and delicious mangoes every year. Even travellers stop here to rest," they said.

But the farmer was not interested in listening.

"This tree stands on my property. I want to use that space for something more profitable," the farmer replied.

He walked towards the trunk and raised his axe.

The Tree's Unexpected Response

Just as the farmer was about to strike, a little boy who had been playing nearby called out.

"Please don't cut down the tree!"

The farmer turned around.

"Why should I listen to you?" he asked.

The boy pointed towards the branches.

"Look at all the birds living there. Where will they go if you cut it down? And what about the people who rest under it during summer?"

The farmer shook his head.

"Those things are not my problem. I need more land for my crops."

The boy looked disappointed but could not stop him.

The farmer raised his axe again. Before he could strike, a ripe mango fell from one of the branches and landed near his feet.

He picked it up.

It was fresh, sweet and perfectly ripe.

The farmer looked at the fruit for a moment. He remembered how the tree had provided mangoes to his family for years. Even though he had never taken much care of it, the tree had continued to produce fruit.

Still, he held on to his decision.

He placed the mango aside and raised his axe once more.

Another mango fell, followed by a gentle rustling of leaves.

The tree stood silently, offering shade to the very man who wanted to destroy it.

A Moment That Changed Everything

The afternoon sun was growing hotter. The farmer had been working in the field since morning, and sweat was running down his face.

He decided to sit beneath the mango tree for a few minutes before continuing his work.

He placed his axe beside him and leaned against the trunk.

The cool shade brought him some relief. A gentle breeze moved through the branches, and the leaves offered protection from the scorching sun.

A ripe mango fell beside him.

He picked it up, peeled it and took a bite.

It was sweeter than he expected.

For a few moments, the farmer sat quietly, enjoying the fruit and the cool shade.

Then he looked at the axe lying beside him.

He suddenly realised something.

The tree had every reason to be destroyed by him, yet it had not stopped giving. It had offered him shade when he was tired and fruit when he was hungry.

The tree did not know whether he was a kind man or an unkind one. It did not judge him or demand anything in return.

It simply continued to give.

The farmer looked up at the branches. Birds were resting among the leaves, and a squirrel was eating a small piece of mango.

He imagined what would happen if he cut the tree down. The birds would lose their homes, travellers would lose their resting place, and the villagers would lose a source of joy during summer.

For the first time, he began to feel ashamed of his decision.

The Farmer Changes His Mind

The farmer slowly stood up and picked up his axe.

The villagers watching from a distance became worried. They thought he was finally going to cut down the tree.

Instead, he walked towards the trunk and placed the axe on the ground.

"I was wrong," he said quietly.

The little boy looked at him with curiosity.

"Are you not going to cut down the tree?" he asked.

"No," the farmer replied. "I almost destroyed something that has given so much to everyone, including me. This tree has never asked for anything in return. Yet I was ready to destroy it just to gain a little more land."

The villagers smiled with relief.

The farmer decided to protect the mango tree. He even began taking better care of it by removing weeds around its roots and making sure it received enough water during dry months.

As the years passed, the tree continued to grow. It produced more mangoes, provided shade to countless travellers and remained a favourite gathering place for the villagers.

The farmer also began sharing its fruits with others instead of keeping them all for himself.

Whenever someone asked him why he had changed his mind, he would point towards the tree and say:

"Sometimes, kindness teaches us lessons that words cannot."

Moral of the Story

Generosity can change even an unkind heart.

The mango tree teaches us that kindness is not always about receiving something in return. Sometimes, a simple act of generosity can make someone pause, reflect and change their way of thinking.

We may encounter selfish or unkind people in life, but responding with bitterness does not always solve the problem. A generous heart can inspire change in ways that anger and arguments cannot.

Remember: True kindness has the power to touch even the hardest heart.
