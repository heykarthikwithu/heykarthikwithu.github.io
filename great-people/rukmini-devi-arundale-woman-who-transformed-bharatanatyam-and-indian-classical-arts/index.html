Rukmini Devi Arundale: The Woman Who Transformed Bharatanatyam and Indian Classical Arts

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Oct 25, 2026 9:40 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 25 October 2026
Rukmini Devi Arundale: The Woman Who Transformed Bharatanatyam and Indian Classical Arts

Rukmini Devi Arundale was more than a celebrated Bharatanatyam dancer. She was a visionary who helped transform the place of Indian classical dance in society, established one of India's most respected cultural institutions and championed traditional arts, education and animal welfare. At a time when women faced significant social restrictions, she followed her artistic convictions and helped bring India's cultural heritage to a wider audience.

Born in Madurai in 1904, Rukmini Devi became an influential dancer, choreographer, educationist and cultural leader. Her greatest contribution was not limited to her performances. Through Kalakshetra, the institution she founded in Chennai, she created a lasting space where dance, music, traditional crafts and artistic discipline could flourish.

Her life also took her into public service. In 1952, she became the first woman to be nominated to the Rajya Sabha, India's Upper House of Parliament. She subsequently played an important role in advancing animal-welfare legislation and became the founding chairperson of the Animal Welfare Board of India.

Her journey offers an inspiring lesson in how one person's vision can influence art, education and society.

Early Life and Family Background

Rukmini Devi was born on February 29, 1904, in Madurai, Tamil Nadu, then part of the Madras Presidency. Her father, Neelakanta Sastri, was an engineer, and her family valued education, Indian philosophy and cultural learning.

Her early years were shaped by the ideas of the Theosophical Society, a movement that explored spiritual traditions and the shared foundations of different religions. Her family became closely associated with the society's centre at Adyar, in present-day Chennai.

This environment exposed Rukmini Devi to people from different countries, artistic traditions and intellectual backgrounds. It encouraged her to look beyond conventional boundaries and develop a wider understanding of culture.

She was not born into a career as a professional dancer, and her journey towards Bharatanatyam was far from conventional. Yet her early exposure to philosophy, theatre and international cultural exchange would later influence the way she approached Indian classical arts.

How Anna Pavlova Inspired Her Journey into Dance

One of the important turning points in Rukmini Devi Arundale's life was her encounter with the celebrated Russian ballerina Anna Pavlova.

Rukmini Devi was deeply impressed by Pavlova's stage presence, grace and dedication to dance. Through this exposure, she became interested in ballet and received instruction from Cleo Nordi, a dancer associated with Pavlova.

However, her artistic journey eventually took a different direction. Rather than building a career around Western ballet, she turned her attention towards India's own classical dance traditions.

She began studying Bharatanatyam and trained under respected practitioners, including Mylapore Gowri Ammal and the renowned dance guru Pandanallur Meenakshisundaram Pillai.

For a woman of her social background during that period, entering the world of Bharatanatyam was a significant decision. The dance form, then commonly known as sadir, faced social prejudice and was associated with hereditary communities of performers. These prejudices contributed to the marginalisation of an artistic tradition that had been preserved and developed over generations.

Rukmini Devi chose to learn the art seriously and present it to audiences who might otherwise have dismissed it. Her public debut in 1935 became an important milestone in her artistic journey.

Rukmini Devi Arundale and the Revival of Bharatanatyam

The revival and transformation of Bharatanatyam remain central to Rukmini Devi Arundale's legacy.

During the early decades of the twentieth century, Indian society was undergoing major cultural and political changes. Interest in reclaiming India's artistic traditions was growing, but classical dance faced social stigma and competing ideas about what constituted respectable public performance.

Rukmini Devi helped change perceptions by presenting Bharatanatyam as a disciplined classical art combining movement, music, storytelling and spiritual expression.

She paid close attention to every element of a performance. Costumes, jewellery, lighting, music and stage arrangements were carefully considered, allowing audiences to experience dance as a complete artistic production rather than simply a sequence of movements.

Her work also demonstrated how stories from Indian epics and literary traditions could be brought to life through choreography. She developed elaborate dance dramas inspired by the Ramayana and other classical literature.

These productions introduced audiences to the expressive possibilities of Indian dance while giving students opportunities to develop their skills in performance, interpretation and storytelling.

However, the revival of Bharatanatyam was not the achievement of one individual alone. Generations of hereditary dancers, teachers, musicians and other practitioners had preserved and developed the tradition. Their knowledge and contributions were fundamental to its survival.

Rukmini Devi's importance lies in how she helped reshape the dance form's public image, institutional training and performance style while bringing it to a broader national and international audience.

Her influence remains visible in the Kalakshetra style of Bharatanatyam, recognised for its emphasis on precision, discipline and carefully coordinated stage presentation.

Kalakshetra: The Institution That Became Her Greatest Legacy

In 1936, Rukmini Devi Arundale founded Kalakshetra in Adyar, Chennai, with support from her husband, George Arundale, and encouragement from people associated with the Theosophical Society.

The institution began with a modest vision: to create an environment where Indian classical dance, music and other arts could be studied and practised with seriousness and dedication.

The name Kalakshetra is commonly understood as a place or field of art. Over time, the institution grew into a major centre for Indian classical arts and cultural education.

Rukmini Devi believed that artistic training should involve more than learning techniques. Students needed discipline, respect for their teachers, an understanding of the cultural background of their art and an appreciation of beauty in everyday life.

Kalakshetra therefore developed an approach that brought together dance, Carnatic music, theatre, visual arts and traditional crafts. Its educational environment reflected her belief that art should be experienced as part of a larger way of life.

She also worked to preserve traditional weaving, natural dyeing and printing practices. Her interest in textiles extended to the colours, patterns and materials used in performance costumes, demonstrating how Indian crafts could contribute to the wider artistic experience.

Today, Kalakshetra Foundation remains closely associated with her name and vision. Its continuing work in arts education reflects the institution she established nearly nine decades ago.

A Visionary Choreographer and Cultural Leader

Rukmini Devi was not content with teaching individual dance pieces. She wanted to explore how classical dance could communicate larger stories and emotions.

Her dance dramas combined choreography, music, costumes, visual design and dramatic interpretation. Drawing on Indian epics, mythology and classical literature, these productions presented familiar stories through a carefully structured stage experience.

Works inspired by the Ramayana and other literary traditions became important parts of Kalakshetra's artistic repertoire.

Her approach required close collaboration between dancers, musicians, designers and teachers. It also encouraged audiences unfamiliar with classical dance to appreciate its narrative and emotional dimensions.

Rukmini Devi's contribution extended beyond the stage. She helped shape curricula, guide students, recruit talented teachers and establish standards for artistic presentation.

The result was a cultural institution whose influence reached far beyond its immediate location in Chennai.

Her work helped demonstrate that India's classical traditions could remain rooted in their heritage while reaching new audiences and adapting to the demands of modern performance.

Rukmini Devi Arundale and the Fight Against Animal Cruelty

Rukmini Devi Arundale's compassion extended beyond human society to the welfare of animals. She believed that preventing unnecessary suffering was an important responsibility and worked to bring animal protection into India's public and legislative discussions.

Her contribution was not limited to raising awareness. In 1953, she introduced the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Bill in the Rajya Sabha. The proposal sought to address shortcomings in the existing legal framework for protecting animals. Although the Bill was withdrawn following discussions with the government, it helped advance the demand for more comprehensive animal-protection legislation.

A government committee subsequently examined the issue, and the legislative process eventually culminated in the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. The Act established a legal framework to prevent unnecessary pain and suffering to animals.

In 1962, the Animal Welfare Board of India was established under Section 4 of the Act, with Rukmini Devi Arundale serving as its founding chairperson. The Board was created to promote animal welfare, advise the government on animal-protection matters and support the implementation of relevant laws.

She remained deeply involved in the Board's work for many years. Her commitment reflected the belief that a society's values should be visible not only in its artistic achievements but also in the way it treats living creatures.

Rukmini Devi was also a committed vegetarian and advocated compassion towards animals in everyday life. Her work helped connect animal welfare with broader discussions about ethics, responsibility and humane treatment.

Her contribution to animal welfare reveals another dimension of her life. The woman remembered for transforming the public appreciation of Bharatanatyam also helped advance a national conversation about compassion and the protection of animals.

Rukmini Devi Arundale: The First Woman Nominated to the Rajya Sabha

Rukmini Devi Arundale's contribution to India extended well beyond dance and cultural education. In 1952, she became the first woman to be nominated to the Rajya Sabha, India's Upper House of Parliament. She served from 1952 to 1962, bringing the perspectives of an artist, educationist and humanitarian into national public life.

Her nomination was historically significant because it demonstrated how people with experience in art and social service could contribute to the country's public institutions. The Rajya Sabha provides for the nomination of individuals with special knowledge or practical experience in fields including literature, science, art and social service.

Rukmini Devi used her public position to advance causes that mattered deeply to her, particularly animal welfare. Her involvement in the campaign for stronger animal-protection legislation showed how parliamentary participation could help bring humanitarian concerns into the legislative process.

She received the Padma Bhushan in 1956 for her contribution to Indian culture. She also received the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award in 1957 and was later honoured with the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship.

In 1977, Prime Minister Morarji Desai reportedly approached her about becoming President of India, but she declined the offer. According to Kalakshetra Foundation's account, she did not want the responsibilities of the office to take her away from the institution and artistic work that had become the central purpose of her life.

Her public life demonstrated that a person could contribute to society in different ways without abandoning a deeply held mission.

Death and Lasting Legacy

Rukmini Devi Arundale died on February 24, 1986, in Chennai, just five days before what would have been her 82nd birthday.

Her death marked the end of a remarkable life, but not the end of her influence. Kalakshetra continued to train students and preserve the artistic traditions she had worked so hard to establish.

Her legacy can be seen in generations of Bharatanatyam dancers, in the institution she founded, in the dance dramas that remain part of its repertoire and in the wider recognition of Indian classical arts.

Her work for animal welfare also remains an important part of her story. The Animal Welfare Board of India continues to operate as a statutory advisory body, promoting animal welfare and advising the government on related matters.

Her life invites a thoughtful understanding of cultural revival. Traditions are rarely preserved by simply keeping everything unchanged. They survive when artists, teachers and communities find ways to transmit them to new generations while respecting the people and histories that shaped them.

Rukmini Devi's approach transformed the presentation and institutional teaching of Bharatanatyam. At the same time, the history of the dance reminds us that its survival and evolution depended on many generations of practitioners, including hereditary artists whose contributions deserve recognition.

Remembering her means appreciating both her individual vision and the wider community of artists whose work continues to sustain India's cultural heritage.

Why Rukmini Devi Arundale Still Matters Today

Rukmini Devi Arundale's story is about more than dance. It is about the courage to pursue an unconventional path, the patience to build an institution and the belief that culture can make society more humane.

She helped bring Bharatanatyam to new audiences, established Kalakshetra as a major centre of artistic education, promoted traditional crafts and advocated for animal welfare. Her historic nomination to the Rajya Sabha also demonstrated that cultural leadership could have a place in national public life.

For young Indians today, her life offers a powerful lesson: preserving heritage does not mean treating it as something that belongs only to the past. It means learning from it, practising it with care and passing it on with understanding.

Rukmini Devi Arundale helped give Indian classical dance a renewed place in public life. The artists she inspired and the institution she founded continue to carry that work forward, ensuring that her vision remains part of India's living cultural story.
