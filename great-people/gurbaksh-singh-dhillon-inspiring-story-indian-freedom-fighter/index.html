Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon: The Inspiring Story of an Indian Freedom Fighter

By Karthik Kumar D K | Published: Sep 13, 2026 10:20 AM IST
By Karthik Kumar D K , 13 September 2026
Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon

India’s struggle for independence was shaped by the courage and sacrifice of countless individuals. While some freedom fighters are remembered across generations, others remain less familiar despite their significant contributions. Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon was one such remarkable personality.

An officer of the Indian National Army (INA), also known as the Azad Hind Fauj, Dhillon dedicated an important part of his life to the fight against British rule. His association with Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose and his involvement in the historic Red Fort trials made him an important figure in India’s freedom movement.

His story is one of patriotism, determination, and sacrifice. It also reminds us that India’s independence was achieved through the efforts of people from different backgrounds who shared a common dream of a free nation.

Early Life of Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon was born on 18 March 1914 in Algon, a village in the Punjab region of British India. His father, Sardar Takhar Singh, was associated with the veterinary services of the British Indian Army.

Growing up during the period of British rule, Dhillon witnessed a time when political awareness and nationalist feelings were spreading throughout India. The freedom movement was gaining momentum, and many young Indians were beginning to question colonial authority.

Dhillon developed an interest in military service and eventually joined the British Indian Army as a sepoy in 1933. His early military experience gave him an understanding of discipline, leadership, and the responsibilities associated with serving in the armed forces.

He continued his education and training, eventually graduating from the Indian Military Academy in Dehradun in 1940. This achievement marked an important stage in his military career.

However, the events of the Second World War would soon change the direction of his life and bring him into the heart of India’s struggle for independence.

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon and the Indian National Army

The Second World War created major political and military changes across Asia. In 1942, British forces suffered a significant defeat in Singapore, and thousands of Indian soldiers became prisoners of war.

During this period, the Indian National Army began to emerge as a force dedicated to liberating India from British rule.

The INA later gained renewed strength under the leadership of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose. Bose believed that India’s independence could be achieved through determined political and military action. His leadership inspired many Indians living in Southeast Asia, as well as soldiers who had previously served under the British.

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon joined the Indian National Army and became associated with Bose’s movement.

His decision reflected the growing belief among some Indian soldiers that their military experience should be used in the service of an independent India.

Dhillon took on responsibilities within the INA and became associated with its military organization. He was connected with the Nehru Brigade, one of the important formations of the Azad Hind Fauj.

The INA’s campaign was extremely difficult. Its soldiers faced challenging terrain, shortages of food and supplies, disease, and strong opposition from Allied forces. Despite these obstacles, the army became a powerful symbol of resistance to British rule.

For Dhillon and many of his fellow soldiers, the INA represented the hope of seeing India become a free country.

His Association with the Nehru Brigade

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon served in the Indian National Army during its military campaign in Southeast Asia.

He was associated with the Nehru Brigade, which participated in the INA’s efforts during the Burma campaign. The brigade operated in difficult wartime conditions as INA forces attempted to advance toward India.

The military campaign ultimately failed to achieve its objective. The INA faced considerable logistical challenges, and the Allied forces eventually regained control of the region.

Nevertheless, the participation of Indian soldiers in the campaign had a lasting impact on the country’s political consciousness.

Dhillon’s service reflected the commitment of those who were prepared to face serious personal risks for the cause of independence. His military background and experience made him an important figure within the INA.

Although the INA could not defeat British forces on the battlefield, its activities helped inspire nationalist sentiment across India.

The Historic Red Fort Trial

The most famous chapter in Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon’s life came after the end of the Second World War.

Following the defeat of Japan, several officers of the Indian National Army were captured by British authorities. Among them were Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, Shah Nawaz Khan, and Prem Kumar Sahgal.

The three officers were selected for trial at Delhi’s historic Red Fort in 1945. The proceedings became known as the INA trials or the Red Fort trials.

The officers faced charges related to waging war against the British Crown. However, many Indians viewed them as patriots who had fought for the freedom of their country.

The trials attracted widespread public attention. People across India expressed support for the accused officers, and the proceedings became a major political issue.

Prominent lawyers, including Bhulabhai Desai, represented the defence. Jawaharlal Nehru also appeared as part of the legal team supporting the INA officers.

The names of the three accused—Sehgal, Dhillon, and Shahnawaz—became widely recognized throughout the country.

Public support for the officers demonstrated the emotional impact of the INA on Indian society. Many people saw the trial as an attempt by the colonial government to punish individuals who had fought for national freedom.

The officers were convicted, but their sentences were later remitted. They were released in January 1946.

The Red Fort trials became an important event in the final phase of India’s independence movement. They generated widespread nationalist sentiment and contributed to the growing pressure on British rule.

Life After the Freedom Struggle

After his release from prison, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon remained associated with the legacy of the Indian National Army.

His experiences during the war and the INA trials became important parts of his life story. He later lived in Madhya Pradesh and continued to be remembered for his contribution to India’s freedom movement.

Dhillon also shared his experiences through his autobiography, From My Bones. His writings provide valuable insight into his military career, his association with the INA, and the events surrounding the Red Fort trials.

Personal accounts such as his are important because they help readers understand the experiences of the people who participated in the freedom struggle.

Historical events are often described through political decisions and military campaigns, but the memories of individuals like Dhillon reveal the personal challenges and sacrifices behind those events.

Awards and Recognition

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon’s contribution to the nation was formally recognized by the Government of India.

In 1998, he received the Padma Bhushan, one of the country’s highest civilian honours.

The recognition reflected his contribution to India and his association with the struggle for independence.

Dhillon’s name also remains closely connected with the INA trials, which are remembered as an important moment in India’s movement toward freedom.

His life represents the contribution of the Indian National Army and its members, whose actions helped strengthen nationalist feelings during the final years of British rule.

Death and Legacy

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon passed away on 6 February 2006.

Although several decades had passed since the INA trials, his contribution continued to be remembered by historians and those interested in India’s freedom movement.

His life serves as a reminder that the struggle for independence involved many different forms of resistance. Political movements, revolutionary activities, public protests, and military efforts all contributed to the eventual end of British rule.

Dhillon’s association with Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army makes his story particularly significant.

He also reminds us of the importance of remembering lesser-known freedom fighters. Their contributions may not always receive the same attention as those of more famous leaders, but they form an essential part of India’s history.

Why Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon’s Story Still Matters

The story of Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon remains relevant for several reasons.

First, it highlights the courage of individuals who were willing to take risks for the country’s freedom. Joining the INA meant facing uncertainty, danger, and the possibility of imprisonment.

Second, his life demonstrates the influence of Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army on Indian public opinion. The INA trials showed how strongly people across the country responded to the idea of soldiers fighting for national independence.

Finally, Dhillon’s story encourages younger generations to learn more about the many individuals who contributed to India’s freedom struggle.

Remembering these figures helps us understand that independence was not achieved overnight. It was the result of decades of effort, sacrifice, and determination.

Conclusion

Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon was a brave Indian National Army officer whose life was closely connected with one of the most important chapters in India’s freedom struggle.

From his early military career to his service under the INA and his involvement in the historic Red Fort trials, his journey reflects the courage and determination of those who dreamed of an independent India.

His contribution deserves to be remembered alongside the stories of other freedom fighters. By learning about Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, we gain a deeper appreciation of the sacrifices that helped shape modern India.

His life stands as a reminder that patriotism is not measured only by fame, but also by courage, commitment, and the willingness to serve a greater cause.
